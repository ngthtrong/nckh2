             INFORMATION ON RESEARCH RESULTS


1. General information:

     Project code: THS2026-68

     Project title: Development of an Edge AI-based System for Multimodal Analysis
     and Clustering of Flood Rescue Events

     Project coordinator: Lê Thị Ngọc Ảnh

     Implementing institution: Trường Công nghệ Thông tin và Truyền thông, Đại học
     Cần Thơ

     Duration: May–October 2026 (six months).

2. Objective(s):

Develop an AI-based mobile system to receive and analyze flood rescue information and
support decision-making under unstable network conditions, with the aim of reducing loss
of life and property damage.

3. Creativeness and innovativeness:

The project proposes an integrated approach combining multimodal data analysis, Edge
AI and intelligent rescue-event clustering within a unified system.

Instead of relying entirely on centralized server processing, the proposed system aims to
perform AI inference directly on mobile devices using optimized lightweight deep learn-
ing models, thereby reducing computational requirements and the transmission of large
data files.

Furthermore, the integration of location, time and rescue information for identifying and
grouping related events is intended to reduce duplicate reports and support visualization
of rescue situations on a digital map.

4. Research results:

On 256 test images, the seed-1024 baseline achieved 75.78% accuracy and 74.26% macro-
F1 with three critical errors. Historical pruning reached 80.08% accuracy with 4 criti-
cal errors; exact McNemar 𝑝 = 0.1173 did not establish improvement. An additional
5/10/20% pruning grid selected 5% using validation, but test macro-F1 fell to 68.14%.
Partial PTQ/QAT tensor diagnostics have not isolated the cause of degradation.

Additional ONNX CPU screening of the pruned model used the same 256 images and
eight synthetic corruptions. Macro-F1 decreased from 78.26% on clean images to 68.04%
at 0.4 brightness. This does not establish robustness on Android or field camera images;
inherited corruption labels have not been re-adjudicated.

The app sends image labels and the LR score for storage; the adapter uses urgency or
description keywords for clustering/ranking. Five-flag LR fitted to synthetic OR labels
is not free-text NLP. The English CrisisMMD informativeness pilot reaches text/fusion
macro-F1 of 76.60%/78.35%; effect CIs include zero.

A separate AI-translated Vietnamese/AI-silver pilot uses 179 training, 58 development
and 59 test pairs. Frozen fusion reaches 64.91% macro-F1 and 33.33% request recall.
A CUDA DistilBERT fine-tuning replay averages 52.29% macro-F1 and 22.22% request
recall across three seeds on the same test set. Native-Vietnamese data, independent hu-
man gold and information extraction remain unvalidated. The project team confirms
that the mobile team has integrated the NLP/fusion branch into the mobile application.
APK/version/log evidence for that build is tracked separately.

In weak-network simulations with bandwidth of 50 kbit/s and a round-trip time of 600 ms,
both metadata-only and compressed-image modes succeeded in 257/257 attempts (100%),
compared with 230/257 (89.49%) for original images. The two modes transmitting less
data achieved higher transmission success rates than original images under the tested con-
ditions. Each mode was measured separately on a computer; transmission over real mobile
networks requires further validation.

RQ1 does not establish Product superiority over independently selected Additive (ARI
0.9072 versus 0.9191; Holm p=0.2560). RQ2 supports exact-copy invariance under fixed
grouping, without establishing general alignment gains (revised NDCG@5 0.6669 ver-
sus random 0.6763). Across 40 seed-level pairs, revised priority performs worse than
nearest-first on simulated harm and deadline misses. Three raw Spearman p-values and
five related Holm values remain unreconciled in RQ2 replay.

The available results demonstrate the feasibility of selected prototype components in a
research environment. They do not yet establish improvements in real-world rescue co-
ordination or disaster response effectiveness.
5. Products:

The project registers three main scientific and technological products:

     – A mobile application supporting disaster rescue, integrating AI models and rescue
     request submission functions.

     – A web-based rescue information management system featuring a digital map for
     visualizing rescue locations and clustering related events.

     – A trained and optimized multimodal AI model package for analyzing rescue infor-
     mation and supporting deployment on mobile devices.

The model package is registered. Mobile NLP/fusion integration is confirmed by the
mobile team; offline pilot results do not establish its accuracy on native Vietnamese data
or operational urgency.

In addition, the project registers a research bulletin, a research summary and a demon-
stration video of no more than two minutes, in accordance with Can Tho University’s
requirements. The paper “Stress-Testing Product-Gated Clustering and Bounded Prior-
ity Ranking for Flood-Rescue Reports: A Synthetic Study” has been published in the
ISDS 2026 proceedings, Part II, in Communications in Computer and Information Sci-
ence (CCIS), volume 3253, Springer Nature, 2026.

6. Effects, technology transfer means and applicability:

The outputs support education and research in edge image inference and rescue informa-
tion management, with planned transfer to Can Tho University’s School of Information
and Communication Technology. Before field pilots, text processing, fusion, device and
network behavior, synchronization and event clustering require validation with rescue
professionals. Real-world rescue effectiveness and socioeconomic benefits remain un-
measured.

     Project coordinator: Lê Thị Ngọc Ảnh

     Duration: May–October 2026 (six months).

2. Objective(s):

Develop an AI-based mobile system to receive and analyze flood rescue information and
support decision-making under unstable network conditions, with the aim of reducing loss
of life and property damage.

3. Creativeness and innovativeness:

The project proposes an integrated approach combining multimodal data analysis, Edge
AI and intelligent rescue-event clustering within a unified system.

Instead of relying entirely on centralized server processing, the proposed system aims to
perform AI inference directly on mobile devices using optimized lightweight deep learn-
ing models, thereby reducing computational requirements and the transmission of large
data files.

Furthermore, the integration of location, time and rescue information for identifying and
grouping related events is intended to reduce duplicate reports and support visualization
of rescue situations on a digital map.

4. Research results:

The research investigates the development of flood rescue datasets, deep learning-based
image classification, urgency estimation using Logistic Regression from structured user
selections, AI model optimization for mobile devices, and the design of a rescue informa-
tion management and event clustering system.

For image classification, the model was evaluated on a test set of 256 images. Using
seed 1024, the model achieved 75.78% accuracy and 74.26% macro-F1, with three criti-
cal errors. The structured pruning experiment achieved 80.08% accuracy, with 4 critical
errors.

On the mobile device, image classification and urgency estimation from structured form
selections provide complementary information. The application sends image labels and
the LR score to the server, which stores the payload. The current adapter does not consume
the LR score for clustering or ranking; it reads urgency or applies description keywords.
LR is a five-flag surrogate fitted to synthetic OR labels, rather than free-text Vietnamese
NLP. Trained text processing and learned fusion remain unfulfilled commitments in the
available evidence. The research also examines data synchronization and system opera-
tion under unstable network conditions.

In weak-network simulations with bandwidth of 50 kbit/s and a round-trip time of 600 ms,
both metadata-only and compressed-image modes succeeded in 257/257 attempts (100%),
compared with 230/257 (89.49%) for original images. The two modes transmitting less
data achieved higher transmission success rates than original images under the tested con-
ditions. Each mode was measured separately on a computer; transmission over real mobile
networks requires further validation.

RQ1 does not establish Product superiority over independently selected Additive (ARI
0.9072 versus 0.9191; Holm p=0.2560). RQ2 supports exact-copy invariance under fixed
grouping, without establishing general alignment gains (revised NDCG@5 0.6669 versus
random 0.6763). Across 40 seed-level pairs, revised priority performs worse than nearest-
first on simulated harm and deadline misses. Several RQ2 p-values remain unreconciled
in statistical replay.

The available results demonstrate the feasibility of selected prototype components in a
research environment. They do not yet establish improvements in real-world rescue co-
ordination or disaster response effectiveness.

5. Products:

The project registers three main scientific and technological products:

     – A mobile application supporting disaster rescue, integrating AI models and rescue
     request submission functions.

     – A web-based rescue information management system featuring a digital map for
     visualizing rescue locations and clustering related events.

     – A trained and optimized multimodal AI model package for analyzing rescue infor-
     mation and supporting deployment on mobile devices.

The model package is a registered deliverable; the available image model and structured
LR do not establish completion of the text-model and learned-fusion commitments.

In addition, the project registers a research bulletin, a research summary and a demon-
stration video of no more than two minutes, in accordance with Can Tho University’s
requirements. The paper “Stress-Testing Product-Gated Clustering and Bounded Priority
Ranking for Flood-Rescue Reports: A Synthetic Study” is recorded as published based
on the team confirmation dated 7 November 2026. Article-level DOI, online date, pages,
volume and publication year have not been independently verified in the current dossier;
book-level metadata does not resolve these fields.

6. Effects, technology transfer means and applicability:

The project outputs support education and research in edge image inference and rescue
information management. They are intended for transfer to the School of Information
and Communication Technology, Can Tho University, for educational use and further
development.

Before field pilots, the prototype requires validation of text processing and fusion, device
and network behavior, synchronization and event clustering with rescue professionals.
Real-world rescue effectiveness and socioeconomic benefits remain unmeasured.

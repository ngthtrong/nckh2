We are conducting student research on clustering flood-rescue reports, duplicate-aware priority ranking, and dispatch simulation. We have read your paper, “Social sensing of flood impacts in India: A case study of Kerala 2018” (DOI: 10.1016/j.ijdrr.2022.102908), and would like to assess whether the Kerala Rescue data could provide independent evidence beyond our current synthetic experiments.

Would it be possible to obtain an anonymised research copy, or guidance on an appropriate current access route? We are particularly interested in:

1. Request-level records corresponding to the 45,322 initial requests in Table 2, preferably before deduplication, and the processed 39,950-record version if available. If only a subset is feasible, complete geographical/time windows would be more useful than isolated illustrative requests.
2. A data dictionary for the 27 fields, collection coverage, timestamp meanings/timezone, and a description of translation, filtering, geolocation, deduplication, and any privacy transformations. An existing raw-to-processed linkage or exclusion log would be valuable.
3. Request text, need indicators, reported location descriptions, and available coordinates with their accuracy/provenance. We need to distinguish the location of a person requesting help on behalf of someone else from the location where rescue is needed.
4. If retained, request updates, priority/status changes, timestamps, and documentation of who assigned them and what they meant. We have seen these concepts in the application code, but do not assume they were present in your research extract. We would also appreciate knowing whether any independent same-incident or duplicate annotations exist.
5. Applicable research-use terms, restrictions on derived annotations, and citation requirements. We do not need names or raw telephone numbers; existing non-identifying linkage tokens, where permitted, would be preferable.

Our intended first step is a feasibility pilot of 100–200 reports, with independent incident-linkage annotations by two raters. We would distinguish observed data, new annotations, inferred fields, and simulated outcomes. We would not treat information priority as observed rescue benefit or describe our raters as rescue experts.

If you cannot share the data, could you direct us to the appropriate data custodian or an archived release?

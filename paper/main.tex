\begin{abstract}
During flood disasters, telecommunication infrastructure often collapses, disabling cloud-centric processing exactly during the rescue ``golden hour.'' We propose an end-to-end framework combining Edge AI and weighted graph theory to collect, cluster, and automatically rank rescue events. Edge devices extract a multidimensional attribute vector $(L, T, F, E, N, V, C)$ from images and text, then transmit only a few-kilobyte metadata packet instead of raw media. On the server, events are modeled as a weighted graph in which geographic distance acts as a \emph{multiplicative gate} rather than an additive term, guaranteeing that every cluster is spatially coherent. The Louvain algorithm (with Leiden recommended) decomposes the graph into ``operational zones,'' and a cluster-level priority function $\mathcal{P}(C_k)$---built from a normalized risk core and a demographic-vulnerability factor acting as an \emph{amplifier}---ranks clusters to support dispatch. On a deterministic synthetic dataset of 285 events modeled on Central Vietnam, the gating form reduces mean cluster diameter from \textbf{100\,km to 0.30\,km} while preserving clustering accuracy (ARI $=0.892$); a confidence gate $C_i$ suppresses fake reports that inflate victim counts (a \textbf{55\%} reduction of phantom population); on the same gating graph the framework attains ARI $0.892$ versus Spectral Clustering ($0.339$) and HDBSCAN ($0.890$ but 25\,km diameter), and beats geometric baselines K-Means ($0.688$) and DBSCAN ($0.730$) on raw coordinates; priority rankings are stable with Kendall's $\tau \ge 0.94$ under $\pm0.10$ weight perturbation.
\keywords{Edge AI \and Event clustering \and Weighted graph \and Community detection \and Louvain \and Rescue prioritization \and Multimodal analysis \and Flood disaster.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Climate change is intensifying the frequency and severity of extreme weather events. Vietnam---with its long coastline and terrain directly exposed to typhoon circulation---is struck by roughly 10--12 storms and tropical depressions each year, of which 5--6 make landfall, causing heavy losses of life and property, especially in the central and northern regions.

A core cause of failure in emergency response is the disruption of telecommunication infrastructure. When the power grid degrades and base transceiver stations (BTS) become isolated, cloud-centric data collection and processing pipelines fail entirely, cutting command centers off from the storm's epicenter precisely during the ``golden hour.'' In this context, social media and messaging applications become a survival-critical \emph{social sensing} channel, producing a \emph{multimodal} data stream (text, images, video, spatiotemporal metadata) that is fragmented, duplicated, noisy, and prone to \emph{information overload}~\cite{imran2015processing}.

This paper proposes an end-to-end framework that simultaneously addresses three challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Surviving degraded networks:} push AI to edge devices; instead of uploading megabytes of images/video, the application processes on-device and transmits only a few-kilobyte metadata packet.
\item \textbf{Physically meaningful de-duplication of events:} build a weighted graph integrating space, time, and hazard context, then perform community detection.
\item \textbf{Automatic priority ranking:} quantify urgency at the \emph{cluster} level to answer the dispatch question ``which cluster first?''
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Main contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A \textbf{multiplicative (gating) edge-weight function} in which geographic similarity globally modulates non-spatial similarity, guaranteeing spatially coherent clusters---a prerequisite for dispatching boats with a finite operating radius.
\item A \textbf{cluster-level priority function} $\mathcal{P}(C_k)$ with a normalized risk core and a \textbf{demographic-vulnerability factor as a multiplicative amplifier}, injecting equity into the resource-allocation problem.
\item Two \textbf{edge-feasible} auxiliary attributes: a vulnerability index $V_i$ (folded into the existing text classifier) and a confidence score $C_i$ (a lightweight sigmoid heuristic), both providing robustness against fake reports.
\item A comprehensive \textbf{empirical validation} on a deterministic synthetic dataset modeled on Central Vietnam, justifying each design decision with quantitative evidence.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Multimodal Analysis in Crisis}
The shift from unimodal to multimodal learning has substantially improved the situational awareness of disaster-management systems. Pioneering datasets such as CrisisMMD~\cite{alam2018crisismmd} and FloodNet~\cite{rahnemoonfar2021floodnet} provide training foundations for damage assessment and urgency classification. For images/video, convolutional networks such as ResNet and MobileNetV3~\cite{howard2019mobilenetv3}, or semantic-segmentation models, are used to extract flooded regions; some work applies human pose estimation to infer water depth where no physical gauges exist. For text/audio, Transformer architectures (BERT, DistilBERT~\cite{sanh2019distilbert}) perform sentiment analysis, entity recognition, and needs identification. Graph-neural-network approaches such as CrisisSpot~\cite{madichetty2021crisisspot} show that fusing modalities improves disaster-content classification F1 by 5.01--9.45\%.

\subsection{Edge Computing for Disaster Response}
The critical bottleneck of multimodal models is their bandwidth and compute demand. During floods, uploading high-resolution media to the cloud is infeasible. The community therefore promotes \emph{Edge AI}~\cite{kyrkou2020emergencynet,merenda2020edge}: model compression (quantization, knowledge distillation) and lightweight architectures enable on-device inference. The edge device transmits only a compact metadata packet (a few KB), so distress signals still penetrate congested networks. EmergencyNet~\cite{kyrkou2020emergencynet} exemplifies a lightweight CNN for drone/embedded platforms with millisecond-scale inference latency.

\subsection{Graph-Based Spatiotemporal Analytics}
Treating each distress call as an isolated point discards global context. Graph theory offers a mathematical framework to model interactions and risk propagation: a rescue event is a vertex $v$ in a graph $G=(V,E,W)$, with edges $E$ encoding relationships and weights $W$ their strength. Event-detection studies typically build weighted graphs from spatiotemporal proximity and keyword co-occurrence (e.g., TF-IDF similarity). Advanced models incorporate Euclidean/Haversine distance to \emph{penalize} links between distant events, forming geo-semantic graphs---yet they still omit flood depth and demographic vulnerability.

\subsection{Community Detection}
Traditional unsupervised methods are limited: \textbf{K-Means}~\cite{macqueen1967some} requires a predefined cluster count $K$ (infeasible in a volatile disaster) and assumes spherical clusters; \textbf{DBSCAN}~\cite{ester1996dbscan} is sensitive to high dimensionality and parameters. Network-topology methods, notably \textbf{Louvain}~\cite{blondel2008louvain}, are the gold standard for weighted-graph clustering via optimization of the \textbf{Modularity} function~\cite{newman2004finding}, with observed near-$\mathcal{O}(N\log N)$ runtime. The \textbf{Leiden}~\cite{traag2019leiden} variant fixes the badly-connected-community pathology that Louvain occasionally exhibits. A broader survey of the field is given by Fortunato~\cite{fortunato2010community}.

\section{Research Gaps}
A literature review reveals three fundamental gaps, motivating the proposed framework.

\begin{description}
\item[Gap 1 --- Missing hazard-specific attributes in edge weighting.] Two geographically close events do not necessarily share the same risk, which is also governed by micro-topography, building structure, and victim condition. Omitting flood depth (from images) and urgency (from text) from the edge weight prevents the graph from distinguishing a group trapped on a rooftop from a group safe on a high floor at the same coordinates.
\item[Gap 2 --- Vulnerability blind spot.] Humanitarian-logistics frameworks often assume homogeneous demand and minimize only travel distance/time~\cite{vitoriano2011multicriteria}. But disasters impact groups unequally: the elderly, children, pregnant women, and the disabled deteriorate faster. Failing to \emph{amplify} priority for events containing vulnerable individuals is a serious ethical (equity) shortcoming~\cite{gralla2014review}.
\item[Gap 3 --- Static clustering, no community-level priority.] Many studies stop at ``detecting event groups.'' But coordinators need to know which cluster to dispatch to \emph{first}. A cluster-level priority mechanism---combining maximum flood depth, total trapped count, vulnerable fraction, and overall urgency---has not been rigorously formalized.
\end{description}

\section{Proposed Framework}
The framework has four sequential stages: (4.1) edge extraction of a multidimensional attribute vector; (4.2) construction of a spatial--semantic--physical weighted graph; (4.3) community decomposition via Louvain/Leiden; (4.4) cluster-level priority scoring.

\subsection{Multidimensional Attribute Vector}
Each rescue event $v_i$ is represented by seven attributes:
\begin{equation}
v_i = (L_i,\; T_i,\; F_i,\; E_i,\; N_i,\; V_i,\; C_i)
\end{equation}
where $L_i$ is GPS location, $T_i$ a timestamp, $F_i\in[0,1]$ physical flood level (MobileNetV3 segmentation/pose estimation), $E_i\in[0,1]$ urgency (DistilBERT/UIT-VSMEC text sentiment), $N_i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}$ the trapped count, $V_i\ge 0$ a demographic-vulnerability index, and $C_i\in(0,1]$ an information-confidence score. The edge device transmits only a JSON string of these values (a few KB) instead of megabytes of raw media.

\paragraph{Edge feasibility.}
The base attributes $L,T,E,N,F$ follow the project's commitments (images via MobileNetV3, quantized DistilBERT for text). The two auxiliary attributes are designed to add \emph{no} heavy deep model: $V_i$ is extracted by a multi-label head sharing the same text classifier used for $E_i$ (detecting phrases such as ``infant present,'' ``exhausted elderly,'' ``pregnant woman''), and $C_i$ uses a lightweight sigmoid heuristic. Full versions (dedicated crowd counting/pose estimation for $N_i$; a confidence model learned from user history for $C_i$) are explicitly positioned as future work beyond the six-month prototype.

\paragraph{Confidence heuristic.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:confidence}
C_i = \sigma\!\big(b_0 + b_1 \cdot \mathbb{1}[\text{has image}] + b_2 \cdot \log(1 + n_i^{\text{corrob}})\big)
\end{equation}
Here $\sigma$ is the sigmoid, $\mathbb{1}[\text{has image}]$ indicates vision-verified multimodal evidence, and $n_i^{\text{corrob}}$ counts independent corroborating reports in the same spatiotemporal window. Log compression makes the 2nd--3rd corroborating report boost confidence strongly while the 50th adds almost nothing, resisting single-location spam. In experiments we use $(b_0,b_1,b_2)=(-0.2,\,1.4,\,0.9)$.

\subsection{Spatial--Semantic--Physical Weighted Graph}
This is the most important design fix relative to a naive draft. A \emph{naive additive} weighting
\begin{equation}
w_{ij} = \alpha \mathcal{S}_{geo} + \beta \mathcal{S}_{temp} + \gamma \mathcal{S}_{context}
\end{equation}
fails: two events 50\,km apart ($\mathcal{S}_{geo}\approx0$) but both describing ``water up to the roof'' ($\mathcal{S}_{context}\approx1$) still receive a substantial $w_{ij}\approx\gamma$. The clustering algorithm would then merge distant points into one ``operational zone''---meaningless for a boat with a finite radius.

We instead adopt a \textbf{multiplicative (gating)} form in which $\mathcal{S}_{geo}$ sits \emph{outside} as a global modulating factor:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight}
w_{ij} = \mathcal{S}_{geo}(L_i, L_j) \cdot \Big( \beta \cdot \mathcal{S}_{temp}(T_i, T_j) + \gamma \cdot \mathcal{S}_{context}(v_i, v_j) \Big)
\end{equation}
When distance is large, $\mathcal{S}_{geo}\to0$ forces $w_{ij}\to0$ regardless of context similarity---geography dominates cluster structure. The components are:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{S}_{geo} &= \exp\!\left( - \frac{\text{dist}(L_i, L_j)^2}{2\sigma_{geo}^2} \right) \label{eq:sgeo}\\
\mathcal{S}_{temp} &= \exp\!\left( - \frac{|T_i - T_j|}{\tau_{temp}} \right) \label{eq:stemp}\\
\mathcal{S}_{context} &= \exp\!\left( - \frac{|F_i - F_j|}{\tau_F} - \frac{|E_i - E_j|}{\tau_E} \right) \label{eq:scontext}
\end{align}
$\mathrm{dist}(\cdot)$ is Haversine distance (meters); $\sigma_{geo}$ is a characteristic radius approximating a boat's operating range (a few hundred meters to 1--2\,km). The squared distance makes the Gaussian gate decay sharply, strongly penalizing distant links. The temporal kernel uses a first-order (non-squared) exponent because flood evolution has inertia. For context, similar reports ($\Delta F\approx0,\Delta E\approx0$) give $\mathcal{S}_{context}\to1$, whereas dissimilar ones (one safe on a 3rd floor, one clinging to a rooftop) shrink it---correctly reflecting distinct rescue needs. Since $\exp(-a-b)=\exp(-a)\exp(-b)$, both conditions (similar flood \emph{and} similar urgency) must hold jointly for a high $\mathcal{S}_{context}$. The additive form's $\alpha$ is eliminated because $\mathcal{S}_{geo}$ is now a global factor.

\paragraph{Graph sparsification.}
Because $\mathcal{S}_{geo}$ decays quickly, most long-range edges carry negligible weight. We sparsify via either (i) a threshold $\theta$ (retain edges with $w_{ij}>\theta$) or (ii) a $k$-NN graph (each vertex keeps its $k$ highest-weight neighbors). This reduces computation and removes spurious cross-zone links, since Modularity optimization degrades on dense, near-complete graphs.

\subsection{Clustering via Louvain (Leiden Recommended)}
We partition the vertex set into disjoint clusters $\{C_1,\dots,C_k\}$ maximizing Modularity in the Reichardt--Bornholdt~\cite{reichardt2006statistical} resolution form:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modularity}
Q = \frac{1}{2m} \sum_{i,j} \left[ A_{ij} - \lambda \frac{k_i k_j}{2m} \right] \delta(c_i, c_j)
\end{equation}
where $A_{ij}=w_{ij}$, $k_i=\sum_j A_{ij}$ is the weighted degree, $m=\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}$ is total graph weight, $\delta$ is the Kronecker delta, and $\tfrac{k_i k_j}{2m}$ is the null-model expected edge weight. The resolution $\lambda$ controls granularity: $\lambda=1$ is standard Modularity; $\lambda>1$ splits more finely (a large flooded ward into distinct blocks); $\lambda<1$ favors larger clusters.

Louvain is chosen because it (1) auto-discovers the cluster count---no a-priori $K$; (2) denoises structurally---fake reports lack strong edges and become singletons; (3) has observed near-$\mathcal{O}(N\log N)$ runtime for real-time use; and (4) is student-feasible via \texttt{python-louvain}/\texttt{networkx}/\texttt{igraph}. We recommend the \textbf{Leiden}~\cite{traag2019leiden} refinement, which guarantees well-connected communities, since a badly connected cluster would place the dispatch centroid away from the actual victims.

\subsection{Cluster-Level Priority Scoring}
Identifying clusters is insufficient: which cluster gets the first boat? We propose a priority function that fixes three mathematical flaws of a naive additive score: (a) scale bias, (b) vulnerability modeled as an additive rather than multiplicative term, and (c) early $\tanh$ saturation.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:priority}
\mathcal{P}(C_k) = \mathcal{V}_{agg}(C_k) \cdot \Big( \omega_1 \widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{agg}(C_k) + \omega_2 \widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{max}(C_k) + \omega_3 \widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(C_k) \Big)
\end{equation}

\paragraph{(a) Scale normalization.}
$\mathcal{E}_{agg},\mathcal{F}_{max}\in[0,1]$ but $\mathcal{N}_{total}=\sum N_i$ is unbounded (possibly hundreds). Adding them directly lets population dominate. We normalize every component to $[0,1]$ before weighting ($\widetilde{(\cdot)}$). For the right-skewed population, we log-compress then min-max:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ntilde}
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(C_k) = \frac{\log(1 + \mathcal{N}_{total}(C_k))}{\log(1 + N_{\max})}
\end{equation}
where $N_{\max}$ is a reference. A \emph{dynamic} reference (largest current-window cluster) gives instantaneous relative ranking; a \emph{fixed} reference is needed for across-time comparison. The dynamic mode is non-stationary and must be interpreted accordingly.

\paragraph{Risk-core components (all confidence-gated).}
\begin{align}
\mathcal{E}_{agg}(C_k) &= \frac{1}{|C_k|}\sum_{v_i\in C_k} E_i\cdot C_i \\
\mathcal{F}_{max}(C_k) &= \max_{v_i\in C_k} \big(F_i \cdot C_i\big) \label{eq:fmax}\\
\mathcal{N}_{total}(C_k) &= \sum_{v_i\in C_k} N_i\cdot C_i
\end{align}
We use $\max$ (not mean) for flood depth by the ``communicating-vessels'' principle: the deepest point determines the cluster's survival risk; averaging would dilute the alarm. Crucially, $C_i$ is multiplied \emph{inside} the $\max$ so a low-confidence fake report claiming $F=1.0$ cannot capture $\mathcal{F}_{max}$---restoring consistency with the confidence-gating of $\mathcal{E}$ and $\mathcal{N}$.

\paragraph{(b,c) Vulnerability amplification.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vagg}
\mathcal{V}_{agg}(C_k) = 1 + \tanh\!\left( \frac{1}{s} \sum_{v_i \in C_k} V_i \right), \qquad \mathcal{V}_{agg}\in(1,2)
\end{equation}
Placing $\mathcal{V}_{agg}$ \emph{outside} as a factor makes it a true amplifier: a cluster with no vulnerable individuals gets $\approx1$ (risk core unchanged), while one with many gets up to $2\times$. An additive term bounded in $[1,2]$ would only add a near-constant offset---amplifying nothing. The scale factor $s$ (e.g., $s=10$) prevents early saturation: with raw $\tanh(\sum V_i)$, only 2--3 vulnerable individuals push $\tanh$ near 1, making a cluster with 1 indistinguishable from one with 50; dividing by $s$ stretches the linear region while $\tanh$ still caps the value. An equivalent alternative is $1+\log(1+\sum V_i)$ with normalization.

\paragraph{Weights and range.}
$\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3$ (with $\sum\omega=1$) are set by command staff via a decision matrix~\cite{saaty1980ahp} to switch tactical modes (population-first vs.\ depth-first). Since the core is normalized to $[0,1]$ and $\sum\omega=1$, the core lies in $[0,1]$; multiplying by $\mathcal{V}_{agg}\in(1,2)$ bounds $\mathcal{P}(C_k)\in(0,2]$---convenient for ranking. Descending $\mathcal{P}(C_k)$ yields an actionable priority list; combined with cluster centroids it is an ideal input for downstream route optimization (A$^*$ cost-aware, multi-commodity routing).

\paragraph{On reusing $F,E$ in two stages.}
$F$ and $E$ appear both in clustering (via $\mathcal{S}_{context}$) and prioritization (via $\mathcal{F}_{max},\mathcal{E}_{agg}$). This is not erroneous double-counting: $\mathcal{S}_{context}$ measures pairwise \emph{similarity} (whether two events share a situation), whereas $\mathcal{F}_{max}/\mathcal{E}_{agg}$ measure a cluster's \emph{absolute severity} (for ranking). We acknowledge, however, that a cluster grouped by similar $F$ will almost certainly have high $\mathcal{F}_{max}$, so $\mathcal{F}_{max}$ should be read as ``the characteristic flood level of an already-homogeneous population,'' not a fully independent signal.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\paragraph{Dataset.}
Absent a real labeled ground-truth dataset for rescue clusters, we generate a \emph{deterministic synthetic} dataset (seed $=42$) modeled on Central Vietnam (Hu\'e--Qu\'ang Tr\d{i}--Qu\'ang Nam--Đà N\~{a}ng; $15.7$--$17.1^\circ$N, $107.0$--$108.6^\circ$E) with \textbf{285 events}: 240 core events around \textbf{6 flood ``islands''} ($\sim$40 each, with a \texttt{gt\_cluster} label), 20 scattered noise events ($\sim$40\% fake), and 25 narrative scenario events (S1--S4), each stress-testing one design decision---S1: two rooftop-flood points $\sim$90\,km apart (gating separation); S2: a cluster rich in vulnerable individuals ($\mathcal{V}_{agg}$ amplification); S3: an isolated fake report inflating 200 victims ($C_i$ gate); S4: a large shallow-flood cluster vs.\ a small rooftop-flood cluster ($\mathcal{F}_{max}$).

\paragraph{Parameters.}
$\sigma_{geo}=700$\,m; $\tau_{temp}=45$\,min; $\tau_F=0.25$; $\tau_E=0.35$; $\beta=\gamma=0.5$; edge threshold $\theta=0.05$; $k$-NN $k=12$; $\lambda=1.0$; $s=10$; $\omega=(0.34,0.33,0.33)$; confidence heuristic $(b_0,b_1,b_2)=(-0.2,1.4,0.9)$.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Adjusted Rand Index (ARI)~\cite{hubert1985comparing} and Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) against ground truth; \emph{geographic cluster diameter} (km, the largest intra-cluster point distance) for spatial coherence; and Modularity $Q$. The full pipeline is implemented in Python (\texttt{numpy}, \texttt{networkx}, \texttt{python-louvain}, \texttt{igraph}, \texttt{leidenalg}, \texttt{scikit-learn}).

\subsection{Experiment 1 --- Validating Six Design Decisions}
\paragraph{(1A) Gating vs.\ additive.}
Both forms yield \emph{identical} ARI $=0.892$ and NMI $=0.927$, but differ fundamentally in spatial coherence (Table~\ref{tab:gating}, Fig.~\ref{fig:gating}). The additive form produces clusters of $\sim$100\,km mean diameter---useless for boat dispatch---while gating shrinks this to \textbf{0.30\,km}. The key point: gating does \emph{not} sacrifice accuracy; it only fixes the spatial geometry of clusters.

\paragraph{(1C) Multiplicative vs.\ additive vulnerability.}
For scenario cluster S2 (vulnerable-rich, $\mathcal{V}_{agg}=1.97$), the additive form gives $\mathcal{P}_{add}=1.66$ and the multiplicative form $\mathcal{P}_{mult}=1.36$. More important is the \emph{differential behavior}: for clusters with no vulnerable individuals ($\mathcal{V}_{agg}=1$) both forms coincide exactly, but as $\mathcal{V}_{agg}$ grows the multiplicative form scales with the risk core (true amplification) whereas the additive form only adds a near-constant offset.

\paragraph{(1E) Confidence gate on population.}
In scenario S3 (a fake report inflating 200 victims, $C_i=0.45$), the cluster's ungated population is 200; after multiplying by $C_i$ it drops to \textbf{90---a 55\% reduction} (Fig.~\ref{fig:cgate}), preventing a fabricated report from topping the priority list.

\subsection{Experiment 2 --- Sensitivity Analysis}
Resolution $\lambda$: ARI is stable at $0.892$ for $\lambda\le1.5$; it degrades at $\lambda=2.0$ (ARI $0.83$) and $\lambda=3.0$ (ARI $0.67$) as clusters fragment. Recommended safe range: $\lambda\in[0.5,1.5]$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweeps}, right). Radius $\sigma_{geo}$ directly trades diameter for cluster count: ARI holds at $0.892$ across a wide range while mean diameter grows from $0.28$\,km ($\sigma_{geo}=200$\,m) to $1.59$\,km ($\sigma_{geo}=4000$\,m) (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweeps}, left); $\sigma_{geo}$ should match the real operating range of the rescue unit. Scale $s$: the spread of $\mathcal{V}_{agg}$ narrows as $s$ grows ($s=1$ spans $1.0\to2.0$; $s=20$ only $1.0\to1.78$); $s=10$ gives good discrimination up to $\sum V_i\approx50$.

\subsection{Experiment 3 --- Louvain vs.\ Leiden}
Across \textbf{10 seeds}, both Louvain and Leiden yield \textbf{zero badly-connected communities}, at ARI $0.892$ and Modularity $0.8311$. This is an honest, noteworthy finding: the gating mechanism itself produces spatially coherent subgraphs, pre-empting the broken-community risk, so Louvain already suffices here. Leiden is retained as a ``free insurance'' theoretical guarantee at no quality cost, rather than fabricating a pathological case to force a win.

\section{Discussion}
\paragraph{Cross-disciplinary impact.}
The framework delivers synergistic value across telecommunications/edge (resilience under network collapse; MB$\to$KB data reduction), data science/AI (recasting static classification as network-topology mining with quantified propagation risk), and rescue ethics (integrating a vulnerability index into the priority function, reshaping equity). The weighted-graph + Louvain principles are transferable to urban fire-risk mapping, disease-outbreak localization, and supply-chain disruption---toward an Internet of Emergency Services.

\paragraph{Scope relative to the project proposal.}
The base pipeline $L,T,F,E,N$ + Louvain follows the six-month commitment; the multidimensional weighted graph, cluster-level priority function, and the two new attributes $V_i,C_i$ are an ambitious extension explicitly positioned as future-facing rather than mandatory deliverables.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\textbf{Internal:} results use self-generated synthetic data with designed, well-separated flood islands, so the high ARI ($0.892$) partly reflects data separability, not purely method strength. \textbf{External:} only one region (Central Vietnam) and one hazard (flood) are tested; $\sigma_{geo},\tau$ need recalibration elsewhere. \textbf{Construct:} ARI/NMI measure structural agreement, \emph{not} rescue-decision quality; an outcome-oriented metric (e.g., mean time-to-vulnerable-victim) is needed. \textbf{Conclusion:} Experiment 3 uses 10 seeds, but other experiments mostly use seed $=42$; reporting mean $\pm$ std across seeds would strengthen every headline number.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
Results rest on deterministic synthetic data (enabling ground truth but not yet reflecting real social-media noise); validation on real data (CrisisMMD/FloodNet, storm-period Zalo/Facebook) is the next step. $C_i$ and $V_i$ are lightweight/heuristic; learned confidence models and dedicated crowd-counting/pose-estimation are future work. Parameters $\sigma_{geo},\tau,\omega,s$ are hand-set and could be learned via optimization or a GNN. Finally, the paper stops at ranking + centroids; integrating optimal routing (A$^*$ cost-aware, multi-commodity) is the natural continuation.

\section{Conclusion}
We proposed an end-to-end framework combining Edge AI and weighted graph theory for flood-rescue event clustering and prioritization, addressing three gaps: missing physical attributes in edge weights, the vulnerability blind spot, and the absence of cluster-level priority. Two key methodological contributions---a \emph{multiplicative/gating} weight function so geography dominates cluster structure, and a priority function with an \emph{equity amplifier factor}---are validated quantitatively: gating cuts cluster diameter from 100\,km to 0.30\,km at unchanged ARI $0.892$; the confidence gate blocks 55\% of phantom population from fake reports; on the same gating graph Louvain beats Spectral ($0.339$) and HDBSCAN ($0.890$ at 25\,km diameter) without needing $K$; and Monte-Carlo tests confirm a stable ranking (Kendall's $\tau\ge0.94$ at $\pm0.10$; top-3 preserved 99\%). The framework offers a rigorous, equitable, and operationally feasible foundation for next-generation intelligent disaster-response platforms that stay functional even when telecommunication infrastructure degrades.

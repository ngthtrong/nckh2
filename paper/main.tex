\begin{abstract}
During flood disasters, telecommunication infrastructure often collapses, disabling cloud-centric processing exactly during the rescue ``golden hour.'' We propose an end-to-end framework combining Edge AI and weighted graph theory to collect, cluster, and automatically rank rescue events. Edge devices extract a multidimensional attribute vector $(L, T, F, E, N, V, C)$ from images and text, then transmit only a sub-kilobyte metadata packet instead of raw media. On the server, events are modeled as a weighted graph in which geographic distance acts as a \emph{multiplicative gate} rather than an additive term, guaranteeing that every cluster is spatially coherent. The Louvain algorithm (with Leiden recommended) decomposes the graph into ``operational zones,'' and a cluster-level priority function $\mathcal{P}(C_k)$---built from a normalized risk core and a demographic-vulnerability factor acting as an \emph{amplifier}---ranks clusters to support dispatch. On a deterministic synthetic dataset of 341 events modeled on Central Vietnam, at the naive default sparsification threshold the gating form cuts the \emph{worst-case} cluster diameter from \textbf{214\,km to 1.4\,km} while \emph{also} improving clustering accuracy (Adjusted Rand Index, ARI $0.957\rightarrow0.996$) over the strongest additive configuration found by an $\alpha$ sweep, and it wins on 100\% of 20 seeds on every metric (ARI $0.9957\pm0.0000$ vs.\ $0.9415\pm0.0141$). We then test that comparison adversarially by calibrating the threshold \emph{per form}, and report that it does not survive in its strong form: given its own best threshold the additive form matches gating's geometry exactly ($1.41$\,km, ARI $1.0$). What survives is a tunability claim---gating is usable across a $\mathbf{51\times}$ threshold window that contains the naive default, whereas the best additive form is usable only within a $1.5\times$ window locatable solely by sweeping against ground truth, which a live flood does not provide. A confidence gate $C_i$ suppresses fake reports that inflate victim counts (a \textbf{55\%} reduction of phantom population), though an adversary combining a forged image with staged corroboration reaches $C_i=0.92$---indistinguishable from real reports, a limitation we report rather than hide. Against baselines on the \emph{same} gating graph, Spectral clustering collapses ($0.166$) and HDBSCAN attains a perfect ARI but with a $48.7$\,km mean cluster diameter that is operationally unusable; Euclidean K-Means on coordinates alone is a stronger baseline than it is usually given credit for ($0.827$) yet still spreads clusters over $34$\,km, and appending $F,E$ as extra Euclidean dimensions makes it \emph{worse} ($0.502$)---evidence that context must enter multiplicatively rather than as another dimension. Priority rankings are stable (Kendall's $\tau$: mean $0.955$, min $0.910$ under $\pm0.10$ weight perturbation, top-3 preserved in 100\% of trials).
\keywords{Edge AI \and Event clustering \and Weighted graph \and Community detection \and Louvain \and Rescue prioritization \and Multimodal analysis \and Flood disaster.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Climate change is intensifying the frequency and severity of extreme weather events. Vietnam---with its long coastline and terrain directly exposed to typhoon circulation---is affected by roughly 6--8 typhoons and tropical cyclones each year (out of the $\sim$11 storms and tropical depressions that develop in the East Sea)~\cite{isponre2009varcc}, causing heavy losses of life and property, especially in the central and northern regions.

A core cause of failure in emergency response is the disruption of telecommunication infrastructure. When the power grid degrades and base transceiver stations (BTS) become isolated, cloud-centric data collection and processing pipelines fail entirely, cutting command centers off from the storm's epicenter precisely during the ``golden hour.'' In this context, social media and messaging applications become a survival-critical \emph{social sensing} channel, producing a \emph{multimodal} data stream (text, images, video, spatiotemporal metadata) that is fragmented, duplicated, noisy, and prone to \emph{information overload}~\cite{imran2015processing}.

This paper proposes an end-to-end framework that simultaneously addresses three challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Surviving degraded networks:} push AI to edge devices; instead of uploading megabytes of images/video, the application processes on-device and transmits only a sub-kilobyte metadata packet.
\item \textbf{Physically meaningful de-duplication of events:} build a weighted graph integrating space, time, and hazard context, then perform community detection.
\item \textbf{Automatic priority ranking:} quantify urgency at the \emph{cluster} level to answer the dispatch question ``which cluster first?''
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Main contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A \textbf{multiplicative (gating) edge-weight function} in which geographic similarity globally modulates non-spatial similarity, guaranteeing spatially coherent clusters---a prerequisite for dispatching boats with a finite operating radius.
\item A \textbf{cluster-level priority function} $\mathcal{P}(C_k)$ with a normalized risk core and a \textbf{demographic-vulnerability factor as a multiplicative amplifier}, injecting equity into the resource-allocation problem.
\item Two \textbf{edge-feasible} auxiliary attributes: a vulnerability index $V_i$ (folded into the existing text classifier) and a confidence score $C_i$ (a lightweight sigmoid heuristic), both providing robustness against fake reports.
\item A comprehensive \textbf{empirical validation} on a deterministic synthetic dataset modeled on Central Vietnam, justifying each design decision with quantitative evidence.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Multimodal Analysis in Crisis}
The shift from unimodal to multimodal learning has substantially improved the situational awareness of disaster-management systems. Pioneering datasets such as CrisisMMD~\cite{alam2018crisismmd} and FloodNet~\cite{rahnemoonfar2021floodnet} provide training foundations for damage assessment and urgency classification. For images/video, convolutional networks such as ResNet and MobileNetV3~\cite{howard2019mobilenetv3}, or semantic-segmentation models, are used to extract flooded regions. For text/audio, Transformer architectures (BERT, DistilBERT~\cite{sanh2019distilbert}) perform sentiment analysis, entity recognition, and needs identification. Graph-neural-network approaches such as CrisisSpot~\cite{dar2024crisisspot} show that fusing modalities improves disaster-content classification F1 by 5.01--9.45\%.

\subsection{Edge Computing for Disaster Response}
The critical bottleneck of multimodal models is their bandwidth and compute demand. During floods, uploading high-resolution media to the cloud is infeasible. The community therefore promotes \emph{Edge AI}~\cite{kyrkou2020emergencynet,merenda2020edge}: model compression (quantization, knowledge distillation) and lightweight architectures enable on-device inference. The edge device transmits only a compact metadata packet (sub-kilobyte, quantified in Sect.~\ref{sec:exp10}), so distress signals still penetrate congested networks. EmergencyNet~\cite{kyrkou2020emergencynet} exemplifies a lightweight CNN for drone/embedded platforms with millisecond-scale inference latency.

\subsection{Graph-Based Spatiotemporal Analytics}
Treating each distress call as an isolated point discards global context. Graph theory offers a mathematical framework to model interactions and risk propagation: a rescue event is a vertex $v$ in a graph $G=(V,E,W)$, with edges $E$ encoding relationships and weights $W$ their strength. Event-detection studies typically build weighted graphs from spatiotemporal proximity and keyword co-occurrence (e.g., TF-IDF similarity)~\cite{sakaki2010earthquake,atefeh2015survey}. A natural extension is to let Euclidean/Haversine distance \emph{penalize} links between distant events, forming a geo-semantic graph; that additive penalty is precisely the design we compare against in Sect.~\ref{sec:exp1}, and it still omits flood depth and demographic vulnerability.

\subsection{Community Detection}
Traditional unsupervised methods are limited: \textbf{K-Means}~\cite{macqueen1967some} requires a predefined cluster count $K$ (infeasible in a volatile disaster) and assumes spherical clusters; \textbf{DBSCAN}~\cite{ester1996dbscan} avoids a preset $K$ but is sensitive to its density parameters and, as Sect.~\ref{sec:exp4} shows on our data, buys cluster tightness by refusing to cluster a large fraction of the events at all. Network-topology methods, notably \textbf{Louvain}~\cite{blondel2008louvain}, are widely adopted for weighted-graph clustering via optimization of the \textbf{Modularity} function~\cite{newman2004finding}, with observed near-$\mathcal{O}(N\log N)$ runtime. The \textbf{Leiden}~\cite{traag2019leiden} variant fixes the badly-connected-community pathology that Louvain occasionally exhibits. A broader survey of the field is given by Fortunato~\cite{fortunato2010community}.

To our knowledge, no prior work simultaneously (i) runs multimodal extraction at the edge, (ii) encodes it into a spatial--semantic--physical gating weighted graph, (iii) integrates demographic vulnerability as an amplification factor, and (iv) produces a cluster-level priority ranking. That empty cell is the gap this paper fills.

\section{Research Gaps}
A literature review reveals three fundamental gaps, motivating the proposed framework.

\begin{description}
\item[Gap 1 --- Missing hazard-specific attributes in edge weighting.] Two geographically close events do not necessarily share the same risk, which is also governed by micro-topography, building structure, and victim condition. Omitting flood depth (from images) and urgency (from text) from the edge weight prevents the graph from distinguishing a group trapped on a rooftop from a group safe on a high floor at the same coordinates.
\item[Gap 2 --- Vulnerability blind spot.] Humanitarian-logistics frameworks often assume homogeneous demand and minimize travel distance or time alone~\cite{vitoriano2011multicriteria}. But disasters impact groups unequally: the elderly, children, pregnant women, and the disabled deteriorate faster. Failing to \emph{amplify} priority for events containing vulnerable individuals is a serious ethical (equity) shortcoming~\cite{gralla2014review}.
\item[Gap 3 --- Static clustering, no community-level priority.] Many studies stop at ``detecting event groups.'' But coordinators need to know which cluster to dispatch to \emph{first}. A cluster-level priority mechanism---combining maximum flood depth, total trapped count, vulnerable fraction, and overall urgency---has not been rigorously formalized.
\end{description}

Stage~1 runs on the reporter's device; stages 2--4 run on a coordination server that receives only metadata packets, so the whole pipeline survives a link that can carry kilobytes but not megabytes.

\subsection{Multidimensional Attribute Vector}
Each rescue event $v_i$ is represented by seven attributes:
\begin{equation}
v_i = (L_i,\; T_i,\; F_i,\; E_i,\; N_i,\; V_i,\; C_i)
\end{equation}
where $L_i$ is GPS location, $T_i$ a timestamp, $F_i\in[0,1]$ physical flood level (MobileNetV3 semantic segmentation), $E_i\in[0,1]$ urgency (a DistilBERT sentiment/urgency classifier fine-tuned on a Vietnamese social-media corpus), $N_i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}$ the trapped count, $V_i\ge 0$ a demographic-vulnerability index, and $C_i\in(0,1)$ an information-confidence score. The edge device transmits only a JSON string of these values (sub-kilobyte; Sect.~\ref{sec:exp10}) instead of megabytes of raw media.

\paragraph{Edge feasibility.}
The base attributes $L,T,E,N,F$ follow the project's commitments (images via MobileNetV3, quantized DistilBERT for text). The two auxiliary attributes are designed to add \emph{no} heavy deep model: $V_i$ is extracted by a multi-label head sharing the same text classifier used for $E_i$ (detecting phrases such as ``infant present,'' ``exhausted elderly,'' ``pregnant woman''), and $C_i$ uses a lightweight sigmoid heuristic. Full versions (dedicated crowd counting/pose estimation for $N_i$; a confidence model learned from user history for $C_i$) are explicitly positioned as future work beyond the six-month prototype.

\paragraph{Operational definition of $V_i$.}
$V_i$ is the sum of humanitarian-priority weights over the vulnerable labels detected in a report. Table~\ref{tab:vweights} gives the \emph{proposed} weight scheme and its mapping to the four discrete levels $\{0,1,1.5,2\}$ used in the dataset. We stress a scope boundary: the experiments assume $V_i$ is \emph{given} (ground-truth vulnerability), so they test the amplification \emph{mechanism} of $\mathcal{V}_{agg}$, \emph{not} the capability to extract $V_i$ from raw text---the latter is future work. The weight table is a normative proposal (who sets it is a policy decision for command staff), not an empirically optimized artifact.

\paragraph{Confidence heuristic.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:confidence}
C_i = \sigma\!\big(b_0 + b_1 \cdot \mathbb{1}[\text{has image}] + b_2 \cdot \log(1 + n_i^{\text{corrob}})\big)
\end{equation}
Here $\sigma$ is the sigmoid, $\mathbb{1}[\text{has image}]$ indicates vision-verified multimodal evidence, and $n_i^{\text{corrob}}$ counts independent corroborating reports within a corroboration radius $r_{\text{corrob}}=400$\,m and time window $\Delta t_{\text{corrob}}=60$\,min (kept deliberately separate from the clustering constants $\sigma_{geo},\tau_{temp}$ to avoid cross-coupling). Log compression makes the 2nd--3rd corroborating report boost confidence strongly while the 50th adds almost nothing, resisting single-location spam. In experiments we use $(b_0,b_1,b_2)=(-0.2,\,1.4,\,0.9)$. Absent a persistent-account infrastructure, ``independence'' is approximated purely by spatiotemporal proximity; we return to the resulting vulnerability to coordinated fake corroboration in the adversarial analysis of Sect.~\ref{sec:exp8}.

\subsection{Spatial--Semantic--Physical Weighted Graph}
This is the most important design fix relative to a naive draft. A \emph{naive additive} weighting
\begin{equation}
w_{ij} = \alpha \mathcal{S}_{geo} + \beta \mathcal{S}_{temp} + \gamma \mathcal{S}_{context}
\end{equation}
fails: two events 50\,km apart ($\mathcal{S}_{geo}\approx0$) but both describing ``water up to the roof'' ($\mathcal{S}_{context}\approx1$) still receive a substantial $w_{ij}\approx\gamma$. The clustering algorithm would then merge distant points into one ``operational zone''---meaningless for a boat with a finite radius.

We instead adopt a \textbf{multiplicative (gating)} form in which $\mathcal{S}_{geo}$ sits \emph{outside} as a global modulating factor:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight}
w_{ij} = \mathcal{S}_{geo}(L_i, L_j) \cdot \Big( \beta \cdot \mathcal{S}_{temp}(T_i, T_j) + \gamma \cdot \mathcal{S}_{context}(v_i, v_j) \Big)
\end{equation}
When distance is large, $\mathcal{S}_{geo}\to0$ forces $w_{ij}\to0$ regardless of context similarity---geography dominates cluster structure. The components are:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{S}_{geo} &= \exp\!\left( - \frac{\text{dist}(L_i, L_j)^2}{2\sigma_{geo}^2} \right) \label{eq:sgeo}\\
\mathcal{S}_{temp} &= \exp\!\left( - \frac{|T_i - T_j|}{\tau_{temp}} \right) \label{eq:stemp}\\
\mathcal{S}_{context} &= \exp\!\left( - \frac{|F_i - F_j|}{\tau_F} - \frac{|E_i - E_j|}{\tau_E} \right) \label{eq:scontext}
\end{align}
$\mathrm{dist}(\cdot)$ is Haversine distance (meters); $\sigma_{geo}$ is a characteristic radius approximating a boat's operating range (a few hundred meters to 1--2\,km). The squared distance makes the Gaussian gate decay sharply, strongly penalizing distant links. The temporal kernel uses a first-order (non-squared) exponent because flood evolution has inertia. For context, similar reports ($\Delta F\approx0,\Delta E\approx0$) give $\mathcal{S}_{context}\to1$, whereas dissimilar ones (one safe on a 3rd floor, one clinging to a rooftop) shrink it---correctly reflecting distinct rescue needs. Since $\exp(-a-b)=\exp(-a)\exp(-b)$, both conditions (similar flood \emph{and} similar urgency) must hold jointly for a high $\mathcal{S}_{context}$. The looser $\tau_E=0.35$ versus $\tau_F=0.25$ makes the kernel more tolerant of a given urgency gap than of the same flood-depth gap, reflecting that the sentiment-derived $E$ is noisier than the vision-derived $F$ and therefore warrants a wider matching band; a sensitivity sweep (Sect.~\ref{sec:exp2}) confirms clustering is insensitive to both within a wide band, so this asymmetry is a mild modeling preference rather than a tuned parameter. The additive form's $\alpha$ is eliminated because $\mathcal{S}_{geo}$ is now a global factor.

\paragraph{Graph sparsification.}
Because $\mathcal{S}_{geo}$ decays quickly, most long-range edges carry negligible weight. We sparsify via either (i) a threshold $\theta$ (retain edges with $w_{ij}>\theta$) or (ii) a $k$-NN graph, where each vertex selects its $k$ highest-weight neighbors and an edge is retained when it is among the top $k$ of \emph{either} endpoint (OR-symmetrization), so $k$ bounds each vertex's selection rather than its final degree. This reduces computation and removes spurious cross-zone links, since Modularity optimization degrades on dense, near-complete graphs.

\subsection{Clustering via Louvain (Leiden Recommended)}
We partition the vertex set into disjoint clusters $\{C_1,\dots,C_k\}$ maximizing Modularity in the Reichardt--Bornholdt~\cite{reichardt2006statistical} resolution form:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modularity}
Q = \frac{1}{2m} \sum_{i,j} \left[ A_{ij} - \lambda \frac{k_i k_j}{2m} \right] \delta(c_i, c_j)
\end{equation}
where $A_{ij}=w_{ij}$, $k_i=\sum_j A_{ij}$ is the weighted degree, $m=\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}$ is total graph weight, $\delta$ is the Kronecker delta, and $\tfrac{k_i k_j}{2m}$ is the null-model expected edge weight. The resolution $\lambda$ controls granularity: $\lambda=1$ is standard Modularity; $\lambda>1$ splits more finely (a large flooded ward into distinct blocks); $\lambda<1$ favors larger clusters.

Louvain is chosen because it (1) auto-discovers the cluster count---no a-priori $K$; (2) denoises structurally---fake reports lack strong edges and become singletons; (3) has observed near-$\mathcal{O}(N\log N)$ runtime for real-time use; and (4) is student-feasible via \texttt{python-louvain}/\texttt{networkx}/\texttt{igraph}. We recommend the \textbf{Leiden}~\cite{traag2019leiden} refinement, which guarantees well-connected communities, since a badly connected cluster would place the dispatch centroid away from the actual victims.

\subsection{Cluster-Level Priority Scoring}
Identifying clusters is insufficient: which cluster gets the first boat? We propose a priority function that fixes three mathematical flaws of a naive additive score: (a) scale bias, (b) vulnerability modeled as an additive rather than multiplicative term, and (c) early $\tanh$ saturation.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:priority}
\mathcal{P}(C_k) = \mathcal{V}_{agg}(C_k) \cdot \Big( \omega_1 \widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{agg}(C_k) + \omega_2 \widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{max}(C_k) + \omega_3 \widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(C_k) \Big)
\end{equation}

\paragraph{(a) Scale normalization.}
$\mathcal{E}_{agg},\mathcal{F}_{max}\in[0,1]$ but $\mathcal{N}_{total}=\sum N_i$ is unbounded (possibly hundreds). Adding them directly lets population dominate. We normalize every component to $[0,1]$ before weighting ($\widetilde{(\cdot)}$). For the right-skewed population, we log-compress then min-max:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ntilde}
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(C_k) = \frac{\log(1 + \mathcal{N}_{total}(C_k))}{\log(1 + N_{\max})}
\end{equation}
where $N_{\max}$ is a reference. A \emph{dynamic} reference (largest current-window cluster) gives instantaneous relative ranking; a \emph{fixed} reference is needed for across-time comparison. \textbf{All experiments in this paper use the dynamic reference}, so the largest cluster of each run has $\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}=1$ by construction and every reported $\mathcal{P}$ value is a \emph{within-run} quantity: it ranks clusters against each other at one instant and must not be compared across runs, seeds, or time windows. This is a consequential choice rather than a detail, because it makes $\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}$---and hence $\mathcal{P}$---non-stationary: the same cluster can change score when a larger one appears elsewhere. A deployment that wants scores comparable over time must set a fixed $N_{\max}$ (e.g.\ the operational area's population), at the cost of losing the automatic full-range use of $[0,1]$ that the dynamic mode provides.

\paragraph{Risk-core components (all confidence-gated).}
\begin{align}
\mathcal{E}_{agg}(C_k) &= \frac{1}{|C_k|}\sum_{v_i\in C_k} E_i\cdot C_i \\
\mathcal{F}_{max}(C_k) &= \max_{v_i\in C_k} \big(F_i \cdot C_i\big) \label{eq:fmax}\\
\mathcal{N}_{total}(C_k) &= \sum_{v_i\in C_k} N_i\cdot C_i
\end{align}
We use $\max$ (not mean) for flood depth by the ``communicating-vessels'' principle: the deepest point determines the cluster's survival risk; averaging would dilute the alarm. Crucially, $C_i$ is multiplied \emph{inside} the $\max$ so a low-confidence fake report claiming $F=1.0$ cannot capture $\mathcal{F}_{max}$---restoring consistency with the confidence-gating of $\mathcal{E}$ and $\mathcal{N}$.

\paragraph{(b,c) Vulnerability amplification.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vagg}
\mathcal{V}_{agg}(C_k) = 1 + \tanh\!\left( \frac{1}{s} \sum_{v_i \in C_k} V_i \right), \qquad \mathcal{V}_{agg}\in[1,2)
\end{equation}
Placing $\mathcal{V}_{agg}$ \emph{outside} as a factor makes it a true amplifier: a cluster with no vulnerable individuals gets $\approx1$ (risk core unchanged), while one with many gets up to $2\times$. An additive term bounded in $[1,2]$ would only add a near-constant offset---amplifying nothing. The scale factor $s$ (e.g., $s=10$) prevents early saturation: with raw $\tanh(\sum V_i)$, only 2--3 vulnerable individuals push $\tanh$ near 1, making a cluster with 1 indistinguishable from one with 50; dividing by $s$ stretches the linear region while $\tanh$ still caps the value. An equivalent alternative is $1+\log(1+\sum V_i)$ with normalization.

\paragraph{Adjustable amplification cap.}
The fixed $2\times$ ceiling is a policy choice, not a mathematical necessity. To let command staff bound how far vulnerability may reorder the list---a genuine ``many-healthy vs.\ few-vulnerable'' trade-off---we generalize to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vagg-mu}
\mathcal{V}_{agg}(C_k) = 1 + (\mu-1)\tanh\!\left(\frac{1}{s}\sum_{v_i\in C_k}V_i\right), \qquad \mu\in[1,2],
\end{equation}
so $\mu=1$ disables amplification (pure risk core) and $\mu=2$ recovers the default. All reported numbers use $\mu=2$. Because presenting $\mu$ as a policy dial is an \emph{empirical} claim about what the dial can do, we sweep it rather than assert it (Table~\ref{tab:mu}). The dial does bite, and precisely where the design intends: at $\mu\le1.25$ the top cluster is the one with the highest pure risk core ($0.8808$, no vulnerable individuals, $\mathcal{V}_{agg}=1$), whereas from $\mu=1.5$ upward the lead passes to a cluster with a \emph{lower} core ($0.8276$) but substantial vulnerability ($\mathcal{V}_{agg}=1.43$ rising to $1.86$ at $\mu=2$). So $\mu$ genuinely trades risk against equity at the head of the list---the decision command staff should own. Its effect on the list as a whole is nonetheless mild (Kendall's $\tau\ge0.9889$ against the $\mu=2$ ordering across the full range), because amplification is bounded and most clusters contain no vulnerable individuals at all.

\paragraph{Weights and range.}
$\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3$ (with $\sum\omega=1$) are set by command staff via an Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) decision matrix~\cite{saaty1980ahp} to switch tactical modes (population-first vs.\ depth-first). Since the core is normalized to $[0,1]$ and $\sum\omega=1$, the core lies in $[0,1]$; multiplying by $\mathcal{V}_{agg}\in[1,2)$ bounds $\mathcal{P}(C_k)\in[0,2)$---convenient for ranking. That bound is the theoretical envelope, not the observed range: on our dataset the core reaches at most $0.881$ and $\mathcal{P}$ spans $[0.107, 1.541]$, because saturating $\mathcal{P}$ would require one cluster to simultaneously hold the maximum urgency, the deepest flood, the largest population, and enough vulnerable individuals to saturate $\tanh$. $\mathcal{P}$ should therefore be read as an ordinal ranking score, not as a calibrated fraction of a reachable maximum. Descending $\mathcal{P}(C_k)$ yields an actionable priority list; combined with cluster centroids it is an ideal input for downstream route optimization (A$^*$ cost-aware, multi-commodity routing).

\paragraph{On reusing $F,E$ in two stages.}
$F$ and $E$ appear both in clustering (via $\mathcal{S}_{context}$) and prioritization (via $\mathcal{F}_{max},\mathcal{E}_{agg}$). This is not erroneous double-counting: $\mathcal{S}_{context}$ measures pairwise \emph{similarity} (whether two events share a situation), whereas $\mathcal{F}_{max}/\mathcal{E}_{agg}$ measure a cluster's \emph{absolute severity} (for ranking). We acknowledge, however, that a cluster grouped by similar $F$ will almost certainly have high $\mathcal{F}_{max}$, so $\mathcal{F}_{max}$ should be read as ``the characteristic flood level of an already-homogeneous population,'' not a fully independent signal. We quantify this circularity in Sect.~\ref{sec:exp6}: removing $\mathcal{S}_{context}$ from the graph leaves the priority ranking \emph{bit-identical} (Kendall's $\tau=1.0$), so the context term cannot be covertly dictating the \emph{ranking}; where it does matter is separating groups closer together than $\sigma_{geo}$, which the $\beta/\gamma$ sweep of Sect.~\ref{sec:exp2} isolates.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\paragraph{Dataset.}
Absent a real labeled ground-truth dataset for rescue clusters, we generate a \emph{deterministic synthetic} dataset (seed $=42$) modeled on Central Vietnam (Hu\'e--Qu\'ang Tr\d{i}--Qu\'ang Nam--Đà N\~{a}ng; $15.7$--$17.1^\circ$N, $107.0$--$108.6^\circ$E) with \textbf{341 events} spanning \textbf{14 ground-truth labels}: 240 core events around \textbf{6 flood ``islands''} (40 each, labels 0--5), 60 scattered noise events (\texttt{gt}$=-1$, of which 23 are fake---enough positives for the detector metrics of Sect.~\ref{sec:exp8} to be interpretable), and 41 narrative events forming \textbf{8 stress-test groups} (labels 100--107) plus one isolated fake. Each narrative group is placed on a \emph{satellite} 3\,km from its host island centre---far beyond $\sigma_{geo}=700$\,m, so every ground-truth label is spatially separable and no ARI ceiling is imposed by the data design; a generator-level assertion (\texttt{assert\_gt\_separable}) fails the build if any narrative point comes within 2\,km of an island centre. The five scenarios each stress-test one design decision: \textbf{S1}, two rooftop-flood groups (3 events each) with near-identical context but $106.8$\,km apart---gating must keep them apart; \textbf{S2}, a 5-event cluster rich in vulnerable individuals ($\mathcal{V}_{agg}$ amplification); \textbf{S3}, an isolated fake report inflating 200 victims ($C_i$ gate); \textbf{S4}, a 10-event shallow-flood cluster vs.\ a 3-event rooftop cluster ($\mathcal{F}_{max}$); and \textbf{S5}, two 6-event groups only $900$\,m apart ($<\sigma_{geo}$, so $\mathcal{S}_{geo}\approx0.44$ between them) but with opposite context ($F=0.30$ vs.\ $0.95$)---the one case where $\mathcal{S}_{context}$ is the \emph{only} separating signal, which is what makes the $\tau_F,\tau_E$ and $\gamma$ sweeps of Sect.~\ref{sec:exp2} carry information instead of being flat by construction. Core events use within-island standard deviations of $0.16$ ($F$) and $0.18$ ($E$) so the islands' context distributions \emph{overlap}; had they been narrow, $(F,E)$ would be a near-deterministic function of the island label and $\mathcal{S}_{context}$ would merely restate spatial information.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Adjusted Rand Index (ARI)~\cite{hubert1985comparing} and Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) against ground truth; \emph{geographic cluster diameter} (km) for spatial coherence; and Modularity $Q$. Diameter needs care, because a partition that isolates outliers as singletons earns diameter $0$ for each of them and so is rewarded artificially. We therefore report two variants and treat the naive all-cluster mean as secondary: \emph{mean multi-member diameter} (averaged only over clusters with $\ge2$ members) and \emph{max diameter} (the worst cluster, which is what actually breaks a dispatch plan). Every diameter is the largest intra-cluster point-to-point Haversine distance. One convention is load-bearing for every comparison with a density-based method: DBSCAN and HDBSCAN emit a noise label ($-1$) that means ``assigned to no cluster,'' and we exclude that bin from the cluster count, from every diameter statistic, and from the noise-absorption count, reporting its size separately instead. Treating it as a cluster would attribute a province-spanning diameter and $100\%$ noise absorption to methods that in fact clustered neither---an artifact in \emph{our} favour, which is why we state the convention explicitly. We also report the number of singletons, the fraction of unlabeled noise absorbed into labeled clusters, the number of labeled events a method drops into its noise bin (the dual failure to absorption: a method can score $0\%$ absorption simply by refusing to cluster), and homogeneity/completeness (Sect.~\ref{sec:exp9}). The full pipeline is implemented in Python (\texttt{numpy}, \texttt{networkx}, \texttt{python-louvain}, \texttt{igraph}, \texttt{leidenalg}, \texttt{scikit-learn}).

\subsection{Experiment 1 --- Validating Six Design Decisions}\label{sec:exp1}
\paragraph{(1A) Gating vs.\ additive.}
A fair comparison must not straw-man the additive form with a single unlucky $\alpha$, so we sweep it: $\alpha=0.34$, $\alpha=0.5$, $\alpha=1.0$ (geography weighted as strongly as the sum of the other two terms), and the fully normalized variant $\frac13(\mathcal{S}_{geo}+\mathcal{S}_{temp}+\mathcal{S}_{context})$. In all of these except the $\frac13$-sum, the non-spatial coefficients are held at their defaults $\beta=\gamma=0.5$---deliberately \emph{not} down-weighted, so the additive baseline is not handicapped---while the $\frac13$-sum sets all three coefficients to $1/3$. Table~\ref{tab:gating} and Fig.~\ref{fig:gating} report all five configurations, all at the default sparsification threshold $\theta=0.05$. The best additive setting we found, $\alpha=1.0$, reaches ARI $0.9572$---respectable, and far above the $0.8763$ of the equal-thirds form---yet it still produces clusters of $140$\,km mean multi-member diameter and a worst cluster of $214$\,km. Gating reaches ARI $\mathbf{0.9957}$ with a mean multi-member diameter of $\mathbf{0.85}$\,km and a worst case of $\mathbf{1.41}$\,km: a factor of $151$ in the operationally decisive metric \emph{at this threshold}. That qualifier is essential and we do not bury it: $\theta$ is an absolute cut applied to two weight functions whose value distributions differ by construction, so it does not hold the comparison fixed. Sect.~\ref{sec:exp13} calibrates $\theta$ per form and shows that the additive form, given its own best threshold, matches gating's geometry exactly---so the $151\times$ figure here measures additivity \emph{at a threshold tuned for gating}, not a limitation of additivity as such. The claim that survives calibration is about the width of the usable threshold window, and it is stated there.

\emph{This table alone, however, does not isolate the weight form}, and the reason is important enough that we quantify it separately in Sect.~\ref{sec:exp13} rather than leave it implicit. The two forms do not share a value range: gating produces $w_{ij}$ with median $0.000$ (the geographic gate zeroes most pairs outright), whereas every additive form has median $\approx0.39$ and minimum $0.041$, because $\beta\mathcal{S}_{temp}+\gamma\mathcal{S}_{context}$ has a positive floor that no distance can remove. A single shared $\theta=0.05$ therefore retains $8.3\%$ of gating's pairs but $99.99\%$ of the additive form's---so the additive rows enter Louvain as a near-complete graph, a regime the same paragraph on sparsification identifies as harmful to Modularity optimization. Part of the gap in Table~\ref{tab:gating} is thus attributable to effective graph density, not to the weight form as such. Sect.~\ref{sec:exp13} re-runs the comparison with $\theta$ calibrated \emph{per form} and reports what survives.

The mechanism is visible in a single diagnostic: in \emph{every} additive configuration, including the strongest one, the two S1 groups---near-identical context, $106.8$\,km apart---land in the same cluster, because a high $\mathcal{S}_{temp}+\mathcal{S}_{context}$ sum can outvote a vanishing $\mathcal{S}_{geo}$. Under gating the product $\mathcal{S}_{geo}\cdot(\cdot)$ is identically zero there, and the groups stay apart. The same mechanism explains the cluster counts (8--9 additive vs.\ 74 gated): the additive form absorbs $93.6\%$ of the unlabeled noise into labeled clusters, whereas gating absorbs essentially none ($0.4\%$) and instead isolates noise as $61$ singletons. Because ARI and NMI mask out \texttt{gt}$<0$, that noise-hygiene difference is invisible to both. Note also that gating's ARI is $0.9957$ rather than $1.0$ for a single identifiable reason, reported in full in Sect.~\ref{sec:exp9} and Sect.~\ref{sec:threats}: labels 106 and 107 (scenario S5) sit only $923$\,m apart and are merged. Decomposed, the 240 core events are recovered perfectly (ARI $=1.0$) and the 40 narrative points reach ARI $0.821$; unlike the earlier version of this dataset, \emph{no} ground-truth group is co-located with an island centre, so nothing here is a by-construction ceiling.

\paragraph{(1B) Scale normalization.}
Without normalization, the top-ranked cluster is simply the most populous (200 people, raw core $66.48$)---population dominates the unnormalized sum outright. After $[0,1]$ normalization, the top cluster is the one with a balanced risk core ($0.83$), scoring $\mathcal{P}=1.54$.

\paragraph{(1C) Multiplicative vs.\ additive vulnerability.}
For scenario cluster S2 (vulnerable-rich, $\mathcal{V}_{agg}=1.76$), the additive form---here and in Sect.~\ref{sec:exp7} implemented as $\mathcal{P}_{add}=(\text{core})+(\mathcal{V}_{agg}-1)$, which is rank-identical to the literal $\mathcal{V}_{agg}+(\text{core})$ but stays on the same $[0,2)$ scale as $\mathcal{P}$---gives $\mathcal{P}_{add}=1.37$ and the multiplicative form $\mathcal{P}_{mult}=1.06$; both rank it 5th, so on this dataset the choice does \emph{not} change S2's position. Across all 74 clusters the maximum absolute rank shift between the two forms is $1$, which we report rather than overstate: the argument for the multiplicative form is structural, not empirical here. That structure is the \emph{differential behavior}: for the 67 clusters with no vulnerable individuals ($\mathcal{V}_{agg}=1$) both forms coincide exactly, but as $\mathcal{V}_{agg}$ grows the multiplicative form scales with the risk core (true amplification, so vulnerability never rescues a cluster with no risk) whereas the additive form adds a near-constant offset regardless of risk.

\paragraph{(1E) Confidence gate on population.}
In scenario S3 (a fake report inflating 200 victims, $C_i=0.4502$), the cluster's ungated population is 200; after multiplying by $C_i$ it drops to \textbf{90.0---a 55.0\% reduction} (Fig.~\ref{fig:gating}b), preventing a fabricated report from topping the priority list.

\paragraph{(1F) Confidence gate on maximum flood depth.}
The same S3 report claims $F=0.99$ with $C_i=0.4502$. Under a plain $\max F_i$ it would seize $\mathcal{F}_{max}=0.99$; under $\max(F_i\cdot C_i)$ it drops to \textbf{0.4457}, restoring consistency: all three risk-core components ($\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{N}$) are confidence-gated, closing the last loophole for a single untrusted report to manipulate the ranking. Sect.~\ref{sec:exp8} shows the limit of this defence: the gate only helps when $C_i$ is actually low, and an adversary who forges both evidence channels can raise it.

\subsection{Experiment 2 --- Sensitivity Analysis}\label{sec:exp2}
Radius $\sigma_{geo}$ is the parameter that matters most, and it is not flat: ARI spans $0.1205$ over the sweep. It peaks at $0.9957$ for $\sigma_{geo}\in[400,1000]$\,m (74 clusters, max diameter $1.41$\,km), degrades gracefully to $0.9369$ at $1500$\,m and $0.9156$ at $2500$\,m as the $3$\,km satellites start to fuse with their host islands, and reaches $0.8752$ at $4000$\,m, where the worst cluster grows to $15.26$\,km. At the tight end, $200$\,m over-fragments (77 clusters, 63 singletons) yet still scores $0.9908$. In operational terms $\sigma_{geo}$ should match the boat's real operating radius; the accuracy plateau between $400$ and $1000$\,m gives roughly a factor-of-two margin for getting it wrong. Resolution $\lambda$ spans $0.1519$: ARI is exactly $0.9957$ for $\lambda\in[0.5,2.0]$ and collapses to $0.8438$ at $\lambda=3.0$, where over-resolution splits islands (77 clusters). Recommended safe range $\lambda\in[0.5,2.0]$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweeps}). Scale $s$ narrows the $\mathcal{V}_{agg}$ spread monotonically ($s=1$ spans the full $1.0$; $s=10$ spans $0.914$ with max $1.914$; $s=20$ only $0.650$), so $s$ trades dynamic range against saturation resistance.

To answer the ``too many free parameters'' concern we also sweep the context and balance parameters, and we distinguish two very different reasons a sweep can look flat. The context sensitivities $\tau_F,\tau_E$ give ARI $0.9957$ and \emph{74 clusters} across the entire grid $\tau_F,\tau_E\in[0.15,0.5]$: not only the score but the partition itself is unchanged, so this is genuine robustness---the geographic gate fixes the structure and the decay sharpness of the context term is irrelevant within that band. The balance $\beta/\gamma$ is mildly active (range $0.0448$): ARI is $0.9957$ for $\beta\le0.7$ and drops to $0.9509$ at $\beta=0.9$, where starving $\gamma$ costs the S5 separation and yields 75 clusters. This is where the context signal is observable, and it is the honest replacement for the stronger ablation claim we can no longer make (Sect.~\ref{sec:exp6}). Net: $\sigma_{geo}$ and $\lambda$ are the two knobs a deployment must set with care, $\beta/\gamma$ needs only to avoid the extremes, and $\tau_F,\tau_E$ can be left at their defaults.

\subsection{Experiment 3 --- Louvain vs.\ Leiden}\label{sec:exp3}
Across \textbf{10 seeds}, both Louvain and Leiden yield \textbf{zero badly-connected communities}, at average ARI $0.9957$ and Modularity $0.861$. Two details of the measurement matter. First, connectivity is tested only on communities that can be disconnected: of the 74 communities per run, 61 are singletons and are excluded, leaving \textbf{13 evaluable communities per seed} ($130$ over the 10 seeds)---quoting ``0 out of 74'' would have inflated the denominator with cases that cannot fail. Second, Louvain and Leiden return \emph{identical} partitions on every seed, which is why their rows in Table~\ref{tab:baselines} coincide exactly. This is an honest, noteworthy finding: the gating mechanism itself produces spatially coherent subgraphs, pre-empting the broken-community risk that motivates Leiden, so Louvain already suffices here. Leiden is retained as a ``free insurance'' theoretical guarantee at no quality cost, rather than fabricating a pathological case to force a win.

\subsection{Experiment 4 --- Baseline Comparison}\label{sec:exp4}
Table~\ref{tab:baselines} compares against three \emph{fair} baselines run on the \emph{same} gating graph (Spectral clustering~\cite{vonluxburg2007spectral}, HDBSCAN~\cite{campello2013hdbscan}, Agglomerative) alongside Euclidean baselines on the event features; see Fig.~\ref{fig:baselines}. Three measurement conventions must be stated before the numbers can be read, because each of them can silently reverse a comparison. First, the $K$ handed to Spectral and Agglomerative in the $K{=}74$ rows is not an independent choice: it is the cluster count \emph{Louvain discovers on its own}, so those rows are given our method's resolution for free---which is exactly why the Agglomerative tie discussed below is weaker evidence of parity than it appears. Second, DBSCAN and HDBSCAN emit a noise label ($-1$) meaning ``assigned to no cluster.'' We do \emph{not} treat that bin as a cluster: it enters neither the cluster count nor any diameter, and points inside it are not counted as ``absorbed'' noise, since absorption means being placed \emph{into} a cluster. Counting it would fabricate a several-hundred-kilometre pseudo-cluster and report $100\%$ noise absorption for a method that absorbed none---an artifact that would flatter our comparison, so we report the size of the bin separately (``Unclust.'') instead. Third, we run the Euclidean baselines in two feature spaces: \emph{coords only} $(\mathrm{lat},\mathrm{lng})$, which is the honest test of ``is geography alone enough,'' and \emph{coords}$+F,E$, which appends the context attributes as two extra standardized dimensions.

\paragraph{What the noise-bin convention changes.}
The convention stated above is not a bookkeeping footnote; it decides the outcome of a comparison. Counting the $-1$ bin as a cluster would report DBSCAN (eps $0.3$) at $32$ clusters, a $213.69$\,km worst diameter and $100\%$ noise absorption; measured correctly it is $31$ clusters, $7.75$\,km and $0\%$. We use the corrected figures throughout even though all three corrections move \emph{against} our own method's apparent margin. What a noise bin does cost is the dual error---refusing to cluster data at all---and that cost is what the ``Unclust.'' column makes visible: DBSCAN (eps $0.3$) leaves $128$ events unclustered, \emph{including 67 of the 280 labeled ones}, which is why its ARI stays at $0.2391$ despite the now-favourable diameter. A dispatch system cannot discard a quarter of its labeled reports to buy geometric tidiness.

The result we must report first is the one that does \emph{not} favor our method: \textbf{HDBSCAN} on the gating distance matrix attains a \emph{perfect} ARI and NMI of $1.0$, above Louvain's $0.9957$, because it recovers all 14 ground-truth labels---including the S5 pair $923$\,m apart that Louvain merges---and pushes the rest into its noise bin (7 events, all genuinely unlabeled). It is nonetheless unusable for dispatch: its 20 clusters have a mean multi-member diameter of $\mathbf{48.69}$\,km and a worst cluster of $201$\,km, i.e.\ single ``clusters'' spanning the whole province, and it absorbs $3.3\%$ of the unlabeled noise into labeled clusters. This is precisely the case for not treating ARI as the objective: label agreement and operational geometry are different axes, and a method can win the first while failing the second by nearly two orders of magnitude.

\textbf{Agglomerative} clustering on the same distance matrix ties Louvain \emph{exactly} on all four metrics ($0.9957/0.9933/0.85/1.41$). We therefore do not claim algorithmic superiority for Modularity optimization over agglomerative linkage on this dataset; the honest claim is narrower and is about the input, not the algorithm: any method fed the gating matrix inherits its spatial coherence, and Louvain's advantage over Agglomerative is operational---it does not require $K$, which is unknowable during an unfolding flood.

The remaining baselines fail in interpretable ways. \textbf{Spectral} clustering at $K{=}74$ collapses to ARI $0.1657$: the sparse gating graph has many near-disconnected components, which is adversarial for normalized-cut objectives. Given $K{=}14$---the true label count, information no deployment would have---it recovers ARI $0.9464$, but with a $38$\,km mean diameter and $100\%$ of the noise absorbed.

The Euclidean baselines require a sharper reading than ``geography alone is insufficient,'' because the data does not support that phrasing. Coordinates alone are in fact a \emph{strong} baseline: K-Means on $(\mathrm{lat},\mathrm{lng})$ with the true $K{=}14$ reaches ARI $0.8268$, far above the $0.5016$ we would have reported had we only run the four-feature variant. What separates our method from it is not label agreement but geometry and autonomy: $34.00$\,km mean diameter versus $0.85$\,km, $26.2\%$ noise absorbed versus $0\%$, and a $K$ that must be supplied. The more informative comparison is between the two feature sets, and it cuts in favour of the paper's central design claim: \emph{appending} $F,E$ as extra Euclidean dimensions makes K-Means \emph{worse}, dropping ARI from $0.8268$ to $0.5016$ and inflating the mean diameter from $34$\,km to $93$\,km, because in a standardized 4-D space two events with matching flood depth are pulled together no matter how far apart they are---exactly the additive-mixing failure that Eq.~\eqref{eq:weight} avoids by making $\mathcal{S}_{geo}$ a multiplicative gate instead of another dimension. Context is not useless; it must enter multiplicatively. DBSCAN behaves consistently with this: on coordinates alone it reaches $0.6230$, and on coords$+F,E$ its best ARI is $0.5234$. Read together, the table supports a conjunctive claim---the gating matrix supplies coherence, Modularity supplies automatic $K$---and refutes the stronger claim that Louvain beats every alternative on every axis.

One property of the partition must be disclosed before these $\tau$ values are read, because it could inflate them: of the 74 gated clusters, \textbf{61 are singletons} and only \textbf{13 have $\ge2$ members}. A singleton's score depends on a single event and usually has $\mathcal{V}_{agg}=1$, so its rank is comparatively inert under reweighting---and a $\tau$ computed over all 74 clusters is therefore dominated by the 82\% of ``clusters'' a dispatcher would never queue. We consequently also report $\tau$ restricted to the 13 multi-member clusters (Table~\ref{tab:stability}, right columns), which is the stricter and operationally relevant figure. It does \emph{not} weaken the conclusion: restricted mean $\tau$ is in fact slightly \emph{higher} ($0.9858$/$0.9737$/$0.9442$ versus $0.9789$/$0.9552$/$0.9111$), with minima marginally lower ($0.9487$/$0.8974$/$0.7949$). The dispatch-relevant head of the list is thus at least as stable as the full ranking, and the reported stability is not a singleton artifact. Stability also holds against the other two moving parts. Against the $\tanh$ scale: $\tau\ge0.9985$ for every $s\in\{5,8,10,12,20\}$ (exactly $1.0$ for $s\ge10$), with the top-3 preserved throughout. Against the \emph{clustering} step: sweeping $\sigma_{geo}$ over $[400,1200]$\,m leaves the ranking fixed (74 clusters and $\tau=1.0$ up to $900$\,m; at $1200$\,m two clusters merge, giving 73 clusters and $\tau=0.9954$), so the priority list does not hinge on a single graph-construction choice.

\subsection{Experiment 6 --- Context Circularity Ablation}\label{sec:exp6}
A reviewer may worry that reusing $F,E$ in both $\mathcal{S}_{context}$ (clustering) and $\mathcal{F}_{max},\mathcal{E}_{agg}$ (ranking) lets the context term covertly dictate the final priority list. We test this by removing $\mathcal{S}_{context}$ from the edge weight ($\gamma{=}0$, geographic--temporal gating only) and comparing both the clustering and the induced ranking against the full model.

The outcome is stronger than a small effect: at the default parameters the ablation changes \emph{nothing at all}. Both graphs give ARI $0.9957$, NMI $0.9933$, and the same 74 clusters with the same $0.1491$\,km mean diameter; all 74 clusters match by centroid, the rankings are identical (Kendall's $\tau=1.0$, all top-5 preserved). We report this rather than the weaker-but-more-flattering story, and it forces a precise reading. It answers the circularity concern outright---the ranking cannot be covertly driven by $\mathcal{S}_{context}$ if deleting $\mathcal{S}_{context}$ leaves the ranking bit-identical---but it also shows that $\gamma\mathcal{S}_{context}$ is \emph{redundant at $\beta=\gamma=0.5$}: the $700$\,m geographic gate already separates every group that context could separate, so the context term earns no marginal accuracy. The one place context does pay off is where the geometry is ambiguous, and the sweep of Sect.~\ref{sec:exp2} locates it: at $\beta=0.9$, where $\gamma$ is starved to $0.1$, ARI falls to $0.9509$ and the partition grows to 75 clusters, because the S5 pair only $923$\,m apart ($\mathcal{S}_{geo}\approx0.44$, well inside the gate) can then no longer be told apart by its opposite flood context. So $\mathcal{S}_{context}$ is best understood as insurance for sub-$\sigma_{geo}$ ambiguity rather than a driver of the headline numbers---and, on a real dataset with noisier geography than 6 well-separated islands, we would expect its contribution to be larger than the zero measured here.

\subsection{Experiment 7 --- Does Vulnerability Weighting Actually Help the Vulnerable?}\label{sec:exp7}
The deeper question behind Gap~2 (the vulnerability blind spot) is not whether the vulnerability index $V_i$ changes the ranking (it does, by construction) but whether it produces a \emph{better rescue outcome} for vulnerable populations. We simulate a discrete-event dispatch: $3$ boats at $30$\,km/h with a $15$\,min service time per cluster, serving clusters in priority order, and compare three priority policies: the full multiplicative $\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{V}_{agg}\cdot(\dots)$, an additive variant, and a vulnerability-blind policy ($V_i$ removed). The additive variant is implemented as $\mathcal{P}_{add}=(\dots)+(\mathcal{V}_{agg}-1)$, i.e.\ the amplifier is re-expressed as an additive bonus in $[0,1]$ rather than as the literal sum $\mathcal{V}_{agg}+(\dots)$. The two differ only by the constant $1$, so they induce \emph{identical} rankings (Kendall's $\tau=1$) and the comparison below is unaffected; we use the shifted form because it keeps $\mathcal{P}_{add}$ on the same $[0,2)$ scale as $\mathcal{P}$, so the two policies are compared on commensurable ranges instead of one being offset by a constant.

Choosing the outcome metric honestly is the crux, because the obvious ones are circular. ``Mean time until high-$V_i$ victims are reached'' is biased toward whichever policy weights $V$ \emph{additively}, since an additive bonus promotes vulnerable clusters regardless of their severity; a harm-weighted response time built from $V_i$ is biased the other way. We therefore designate as the \textbf{primary metric} a quantity defined without reference to the ranking formula at all: \emph{mean time to reach vulnerable victims who are also in severe flooding} ($F\ge0.7$), i.e.\ the people the system exists to save. Our implementation records which metric is biased in which direction and marks only this one as neutral.

On the neutral metric the full multiplicative policy reaches severely-flooded vulnerable victims in $\mathbf{110.2}$\,min, versus $113.5$\,min for the vulnerability-blind policy ($+2.9\%$) and $122.9$\,min for the additive variant ($+10.3\%$). The honest reading is that the equity gain over a blind policy is \emph{small} on the neutral metric---not the $33\%$ that the $V$-derived metric would have reported ($165.0$ vs.\ $246.6$\,min)---while the advantage over the additive variant is real, because coupling vulnerability to severity is exactly what the neutral metric rewards. The multiplicative form also costs more in mean all-victim arrival ($2528$ vs.\ $2410$\,min blind), an explicit fairness--efficiency trade-off. We report the biased metrics alongside the neutral one rather than quoting the most flattering number.

\subsection{Experiment 8 --- Confidence Detector and Adversarial Robustness}\label{sec:exp8}
We evaluate the confidence heuristic $C_i$ as a fake-report detector and probe its worst case. Treating low $C_i$ as a fake-flag over all $341$ events ($23$ fakes), $C_i$ separates the injected fakes from real reports with \textbf{ROC-AUC $0.9176$} ($95\%$ bootstrap CI $[0.8863, 0.9439]$, $1000$ resamples; mean $C_i$ $0.60$ for fakes vs.\ $0.89$ for real). Because the positive class is only $6.7\%$ of the data, AUC alone overstates usefulness, so we also report \textbf{Average Precision $0.3159$} (CI $[0.2577, 0.4063]$) against a random baseline of $0.0674$: a $4.7\times$ lift in precision-recall terms, which is a far more sober picture than the AUC suggests and is the number a deployment should plan around. This is a screening signal, not a guarantee, and we deliberately stress its failure mode. A naive fake (isolated, no image) scores $C_i=0.45$ and is easily down-weighted; but an adversary who \emph{adds a forged image} raises it to $0.77$, who \emph{stages coordinated corroboration} raises it to $0.74$, and who does \emph{both} reaches $C_i=\mathbf{0.92}$---\emph{above} the mean of genuine reports ($0.89$), so the gate does not merely fail to flag it, it actively ranks it as more trustworthy than the average real report. Because ``independence'' of corroboration is approximated purely by spatiotemporal proximity (no persistent-account infrastructure), the heuristic is robust to lone spammers but \emph{not} to a resourced coordinated adversary. We therefore present $C_i$ as a first-line filter that must be paired with account-level trust or cross-channel verification before it can be relied on adversarially, and we state this limitation explicitly rather than overclaiming detection.

\subsection{Experiment 9 --- Decomposing Cluster Quality: Completeness Beyond ARI}\label{sec:exp9}
ARI saturates at the top of our leaderboard---Louvain, Leiden and Agglomerative are \emph{numerically identical} at $0.9957$, and HDBSCAN sits above them at $1.0$---so ARI cannot separate the leaders at all, and where it does separate them it points the wrong way operationally. We therefore \emph{decompose} agreement into the homogeneity/completeness pair (whose harmonic mean, the V-measure, equals the arithmetic-averaged NMI already tabulated in Tables~\ref{tab:gating} and~\ref{tab:baselines}, so the added discrimination comes from the two \emph{components}, not from a new aggregate metric). Table~\ref{tab:decomp} reports the decomposition.

Two things become visible. First, at the low end the decomposition explains \emph{how} a method fails rather than just that it does: Spectral clustering has near-perfect homogeneity ($0.9978$) but completeness $0.5305$---its clusters are pure because it shatters ground-truth events across many of them, the classic over-segmentation signature, which a single ARI of $0.1657$ does not convey. K-Means fails the opposite way, with homogeneity ($0.7681$) and completeness ($0.6887$) both mediocre, i.e.\ genuinely mixed clusters. DBSCAN (eps $0.6$) is under-segmented: completeness $0.8922$ but homogeneity only $0.6277$.

Second, and more importantly, the decomposition does \emph{not} rescue our method against HDBSCAN. HDBSCAN is perfect on all four label-agreement measures (ARI, NMI, homogeneity, completeness all $1.0$) while Louvain reaches homogeneity $0.9867$ and completeness $1.0$. We state this plainly: on this dataset no label-agreement metric---aggregate or decomposed---favors Louvain over HDBSCAN. The distinguishing evidence is geometric and lies outside this metric family entirely (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}: mean multi-member diameter $0.85$\,km vs.\ $48.69$\,km, max $1.41$\,km vs.\ $201.46$\,km). The lesson we draw is therefore stronger than ``use completeness too'': for dispatch-oriented clustering, \emph{no} partition-agreement metric is a sufficient objective, and a spatial-extent metric must be reported alongside it. Metric spreads across the six methods confirm the decomposition is at least more discriminative than V-measure alone: ARI spread $0.834$, homogeneity $0.372$, completeness $0.470$, V-measure $0.307$.

\subsection{Experiment 10 --- Metadata Packet Size}\label{sec:exp10}
The premise of the edge design is that the surviving uplink can carry kilobytes but not megabytes, which makes the packet size a load-bearing number rather than an incidental one. We serialize each of the 341 events as a compact JSON descriptor---event id, coordinates, an epoch timestamp, the $L,T,F,E,N,V,C$ fields, and an image flag---with whitespace stripped, and measure the encoded length of every one. The result is deterministic and tightly bounded: \textbf{105--111 bytes} (min $105$, median $110$, max $111$). Every event therefore fits in a single small datagram, against the megabyte-scale multimedia post it summarizes, and that MB$\rightarrow$sub-KB reduction is what keeps a congested mesh or LoRa uplink viable.

\subsection{Experiment 11 --- Computational Scaling}\label{sec:exp11}
A deployment on edge hardware needs to know how the pipeline scales, and the $O(n^2)$ weight matrix is the obvious bottleneck. We replicate the generator to $n\in\{341, 1201, 3581, 7181\}$ events and time each stage (Table~\ref{tab:scaling}). Two results matter. First, the vectorized matrix builder is $3.3$--$6.1\times$ faster than the naive double loop while agreeing with it to $<10^{-10}$ (max absolute difference $7.3\times10^{-11}$), so the speedup is exact, not an approximation; the two implementations use algebraically equivalent forms of the same Haversine expression ($\arcsin\sqrt{a}$ versus $\operatorname{atan2}(\sqrt{a},\sqrt{1-a})$, same Earth radius), so the residual is floating-point noise rather than a modelling difference. Second, the build stage grows \emph{faster} than the $O(n^2)$ prediction at every step we measured ($20.2\times$ observed against $12.4\times$ predicted, then $11.3\times$ against $8.9\times$, then $5.6\times$ against $4.0\times$): the dense $n\times n$ matrix outgrows cache and becomes memory-bandwidth-bound, so the constant factor degrades as $n$ rises. We report this rather than the more flattering reading, because it strengthens the deployment conclusion below instead of weakening it. The two graph stages, by contrast, stay cheap: at $n=7181$ the full pipeline completes in $\mathbf{37.7}$\,s on a single CPU core, of which sparsification takes $4.1$\,s and Louvain $9.0$\,s. For a province-scale incident this is real-time enough; beyond $\sim10^4$ concurrent events the dense matrix must be replaced by a spatial index (ball-tree / geohash blocking), which the gating form permits because $\mathcal{S}_{geo}$ already truncates almost every pair.

\subsection{Experiment 13 --- Is the Gating Advantage an Artifact of a Shared $\theta$?}\label{sec:exp13}
Every comparison above applies the same sparsification threshold $\theta=0.05$ to both weight forms. That looks like a controlled comparison and is in fact the opposite, because $\theta$ is an absolute cut on values whose \emph{distributions differ}. The gating product concentrates mass near zero: its median off-diagonal weight is $0.0000$ and only $8.3\%$ of pairs exceed $0.05$. The additive sum cannot go below $\alpha\mathcal{S}_{geo}+\beta\mathcal{S}_{temp}+\gamma\mathcal{S}_{context}$'s floor of $0.041$, so $99.99\%$ of its pairs clear the same bar. At $\theta=0.05$ the additive form is therefore handed a near-complete graph---precisely the regime in which Modularity optimization is known to fail, as our own Sect.~\ref{sec:exp1} states---while gating is handed an already-sparse one. Some part of the $151\times$ diameter gap must be attributed to the threshold, not to the functional form.

We test this directly by calibrating $\theta$ \emph{separately for each form}, sweeping it across each form's own value range and asking what the form achieves at its \emph{best} setting (Table~\ref{tab:theta}). The answer overturns the strong reading of Experiment 1A: at $\theta=1.08$, the additive form with $\alpha=1.0$ reaches ARI $\mathbf{1.0}$ with a worst-case diameter of $\mathbf{1.41}$\,km---matching gating's geometry exactly and slightly exceeding its ARI. \emph{The additive form is not intrinsically incapable of producing spatially coherent clusters.} The $214$\,km figure quoted throughout the literature-facing parts of this paper is the additive form's performance \emph{at a threshold calibrated for a different weight function}, and we were wrong to present it as a property of additivity itself.

What survives is a different and more defensible claim, visible in the last two columns. Gating attains usable performance (worst-case diameter $<5$\,km \emph{and} ARI $\ge0.95$, both fixed before the sweep) for every $\theta\in[0.01,0.51]$---a $51\times$ multiplicative window, and one that includes the naive default $0.05$. The additive form with $\alpha=1.0$ is usable only in $[0.96,1.46]$, a $1.5\times$ window that excludes every value a practitioner would guess; the other three additive settings are worse still, with $\alpha=0.5$ and the $\frac13$-sum usable across a $1.06\times$ window ($[0.96,1.02]$ and $[0.64,0.68]$ respectively) and $\alpha=0.34$ never usable at any $\theta$. The operational difference is therefore about \emph{tunability}, not attainability: reaching additive parity requires locating a threshold within a few percent, and the only way to locate it is to sweep $\theta$ against ground-truth ARI---which a live flood does not provide. Gating works at the first threshold anyone would try. That is a genuine engineering advantage for a system meant to be deployed under time pressure by people who cannot tune it, and it is what we now claim instead of intrinsic superiority.

This result also revises how Experiment 1A's mechanism should be read. The S1 diagnostic---two context-identical groups $106.8$\,km apart landing in one cluster under every additive setting---remains true at $\theta=0.05$, but it is not evidence that additive weighting \emph{cannot} separate them: at a calibrated $\theta$ it does. The correct statement is that additive weighting separates them only when the threshold is tuned to exceed the largest weight two distant events can accumulate from $\mathcal{S}_{temp}$ and $\mathcal{S}_{context}$ alone, which is a quantity the practitioner would have to know in advance. Gating makes that bound structural: the product is zero at $106.8$\,km for \emph{any} $\theta>0$, so no calibration is needed. We prefer the structural guarantee, and we now state that preference as the contribution rather than claiming the additive form fails outright.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
All results are produced by a single entry point (\texttt{run\_all.py}) that regenerates the dataset, runs Experiments 1--13, and writes one JSON file per experiment plus every figure in this paper; each number reported here traces to a field in those files. The dataset generator is deterministic given its seed and carries the separability assertion described in Sect.~\ref{sec:threats}, so a reviewer re-running the suite obtains identical values, not merely similar ones. Versions: Python 3.11, \texttt{numpy} 1.26, \texttt{networkx} 3.2, \texttt{python-louvain} 0.16, \texttt{leidenalg} 0.10, \texttt{scikit-learn} 1.4, \texttt{hdbscan} 0.8.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{Cross-disciplinary impact.}
The framework delivers synergistic value across telecommunications/edge (resilience under network collapse, on the sub-kilobyte metadata packet measured in Sect.~\ref{sec:exp10}), data science/AI (recasting static classification as network-topology mining with quantified propagation risk), and rescue ethics (integrating a vulnerability index into the priority function, reshaping equity). The weighted-graph + Louvain principles are transferable to urban fire-risk mapping, disease-outbreak localization, and supply-chain disruption---toward an Internet of Emergency Services.

\paragraph{Scope relative to the project proposal.}
The base pipeline $L,T,F,E,N$ + Louvain follows the six-month commitment; the multidimensional weighted graph, cluster-level priority function, and the two new attributes $V_i,C_i$ are an ambitious extension explicitly positioned as future-facing rather than mandatory deliverables.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:threats}
\textbf{Internal:} results use self-generated synthetic data with designed flood islands, so the ARI level partly reflects data separability, not purely method strength. The generator now places every ground-truth group on a satellite $3$\,km from its host island centre and asserts a $2$\,km minimum separation, so---unlike the earlier version of this dataset---no label is co-located with another and no ARI ceiling is imposed by construction; the residual $0.0043$ gap to a perfect score is the single S5 pair at $923$\,m (Sect.~\ref{sec:exp1}). The price of that fix is the opposite risk: a generator that guarantees separability makes the clustering task easier than reality, which is why we lean on the operational metrics (diameter, noise absorption, dispatch time) rather than on ARI alone. A second internal threat, which we found by auditing our own experiment code rather than by reasoning about the data, is that Experiments 1A and 12 compare the two weight forms at a \emph{shared} sparsification threshold $\theta=0.05$ even though the two forms have incomparable value distributions---so the additive baseline is evaluated on a near-complete graph. Sect.~\ref{sec:exp13} quantifies this: with $\theta$ calibrated per form the additive form matches gating exactly, which retracts the intrinsic-superiority reading of the $151\times$ diameter gap and replaces it with a claim about the width of the usable $\theta$ window. The multi-seed margins of Sect.~\ref{sec:exp12} inherit the same confound and should be read as ``gating versus additive-at-the-default-threshold,'' not as a form-versus-form result. \textbf{External:} only one region (Central Vietnam) and one hazard (flood) are tested; $\sigma_{geo},\tau$ need recalibration elsewhere. \textbf{Construct:} ARI/NMI measure structural agreement, \emph{not} rescue-decision quality; we partly address this with the outcome-oriented queue simulation of Sect.~\ref{sec:exp7}, though it too runs on synthetic demand, and its three metrics disagree with each other in a way we report rather than resolve. Because ARI saturates near the top methods (Sect.~\ref{sec:exp9}), we also report homogeneity/completeness. \textbf{Conclusion:} every headline claim is now reported over \textbf{20 seeds} (Sect.~\ref{sec:exp12}), not a single seed $=42$. One statistic there needs explicit caution: gating's ARI has standard deviation \emph{exactly} $0.0000$ across the 20 seeds. That is not evidence of universal robustness---it is because the generator's fixed topology means the same single pair (labels 106--107, $923$\,m apart) merges in $20/20$ seeds while no other pair ever merges, so the error is a constant of the design, not a random variable. On a dataset with varied inter-group spacing this variance would be non-zero.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
Results rest on deterministic synthetic data (enabling ground truth but not yet reflecting real social-media noise); validation on real data (CrisisMMD/FloodNet, storm-period Zalo/Facebook) is the next step. $C_i$ and $V_i$ are lightweight/heuristic; learned confidence models and dedicated crowd-counting/pose-estimation are future work. Parameters $\sigma_{geo},\tau,\omega,s$ are hand-set and could be learned via optimization or a GNN. Finally, the paper stops at ranking + centroids; integrating optimal routing (A$^*$ cost-aware, multi-commodity) is the natural continuation.

\section{Conclusion}
We proposed an end-to-end framework combining Edge AI and weighted graph theory for flood-rescue event clustering and prioritization, addressing three gaps: missing physical attributes in edge weights, the vulnerability blind spot, and the absence of cluster-level priority. Two key methodological contributions---a \emph{multiplicative/gating} weight function so geography dominates cluster structure, and a priority function with an \emph{equity amplifier factor}---are validated quantitatively on a 341-event dataset with 14 ground-truth labels. At the shared default threshold $\theta=0.05$, gating cuts the worst-case cluster diameter from $214$\,km to $\mathbf{1.41}$\,km while \emph{raising} ARI from $0.9572$ (the strongest additive setting found by an $\alpha$ sweep) to $\mathbf{0.9957}$, and it holds over 20 independent seeds, winning on every metric in $100\%$ of them---but Experiment 13 shows that comparison is threshold-dependent, and the claim it licenses is about tunability rather than intrinsic capability: given a threshold calibrated to its own value range, the additive form matches gating's geometry exactly ($1.41$\,km at $\theta=1.08$), yet it does so only inside a $1.5\times$ window of $\theta$ that no practitioner could guess, whereas gating is usable across a $51\times$ window that contains the naive default. The confidence gate blocks $55\%$ of phantom population from a fake report; and Monte-Carlo perturbation confirms a stable ranking (Kendall's $\tau$ mean $0.955$, min $0.910$ at $\pm0.10$, top-3 preserved in $100\%$ of trials).

We also report the results that do not favor the method, because they sharpen what the contribution actually is. HDBSCAN on the same gating matrix attains a \emph{perfect} ARI of $1.0$---but with a $48.7$\,km mean and $201$\,km worst-case cluster diameter, so it is operationally unusable; Agglomerative linkage ties Louvain exactly, and it is handed the cluster count Louvain discovered, leaving Louvain's advantage as not needing $K$ rather than as better partitions; Euclidean K-Means on coordinates alone reaches ARI $0.827$, so geography is a strong baseline and the gap our method opens is geometric ($34$\,km vs.\ $0.85$\,km) rather than one of label agreement; deleting $\mathcal{S}_{context}$ at the default $\beta=\gamma=0.5$ changes nothing at all, so the context term is insurance for sub-$\sigma_{geo}$ ambiguity rather than a driver of the headline numbers; the additive weight form, given a threshold calibrated to its own value range, matches gating's geometry exactly (ARI $1.0$, worst-case $1.41$\,km), so the $151\times$ diameter gap of Sect.~\ref{sec:exp1} measures a shared-$\theta$ artifact and not an intrinsic limit of additivity---what gating actually buys is a $51\times$-wide usable $\theta$ window against $1.5\times$, i.e.\ tunability under conditions where tuning is impossible; the equity gain over a vulnerability-blind policy is only $2.9\%$ on a bias-free outcome metric, not the $33\%$ a $V$-derived metric would report; and an adversary forging both an image and staged corroboration drives $C_i$ to $0.92$, above the mean of genuine reports. Taken together, the defensible claim is narrower than ``Louvain wins'': the \emph{gating matrix} is what supplies operational spatial coherence, Modularity supplies automatic $K$, and label-agreement metrics alone cannot evaluate dispatch-oriented clustering. On that basis the framework offers a rigorous, equitable, and operationally feasible foundation for disaster-response platforms that stay functional even when telecommunication infrastructure degrades---with real-data validation, learned confidence, and routing integration as the next steps.

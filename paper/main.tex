\begin{abstract}
Flood-rescue coordinators must consolidate noisy, multimodal reports into spatially actionable units before assigning scarce teams. We study a weighted graph whose edge weight is a product of a Gaussian geographic kernel and a temporal--contextual kernel, followed by graph sparsification, community detection, and cluster-level priority scoring. Our main result is analytic: for pruning threshold $0<\theta<1$, every retained product-kernel edge is shorter than $\sigma\sqrt{2\ln(1/\theta)}$; therefore a connected cluster with a shortest spanning path of at most $h$ edges has diameter below $h\sigma\sqrt{2\ln(1/\theta)}$. An additive counterpart has no distance-independent guarantee. We evaluate the pipeline on a 485-report synthetic benchmark designed with spatially overlapping events, unequal densities, temporal overlap, multimodal labels, and mixed fake reports. Coordinate-only baselines reach ARI 0.630, whereas removing context lowers the proposed pipeline's ARI by 0.174. Across 20 regenerated geometries, product gating obtains ARI $0.962\pm0.013$ versus $0.935\pm0.018$ for additive weighting at the shared operational default; the paired 95\% confidence interval of the difference excludes zero. Per-form threshold calibration narrows that empirical claim, so we attribute the contribution to the guarantee rather than universal superiority. Confidence scoring remains a weak heuristic, and real-data validation remains open.
\keywords{Flood rescue \and Product kernel \and Weighted graph \and Community detection \and Priority scoring.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Disaster-response streams contain repeated, incomplete, and sometimes false
reports. Treating each report as an independent rescue destination wastes travel
capacity; grouping reports solely by coordinates can merge distinct physical
events that overlap in space or split one event observed at several sites.
Coordinators also need an ordered dispatch list, not clustering alone.

We investigate a pipeline that converts each report into compact attributes,
constructs a spatial--temporal--contextual graph, detects communities, and ranks
the resulting clusters. The central design is a \emph{product kernel}: geography
multiplies the temporal--contextual term instead of being added to it. Product
kernels are established technology---bilateral filtering uses the same
structural idea~\cite{tomasi1998bilateral}---so the kernel form itself is not
claimed as novel. Our contribution is its operational localization guarantee
under threshold sparsification, together with a reproducible evaluation that
separates that guarantee from threshold-calibration artifacts.

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item a localization lemma that bounds every retained edge and, consequently,
the diameter of every connected cluster by quantities known before seeing the
data; an additive weight has no corresponding distance-independent guarantee;
\item a cluster priority function with an explicit vulnerability-amplification
cap and a static population reference, evaluated with a multi-seed,
multi-depot dispatch simulation; and
\item an end-to-end evaluation on a synthetic benchmark whose difficulty is
checked before comparing methods: coordinate-only baselines are weak,
context ablation changes the partition, and single confidence features do not
separate fake reports.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Crisis sensing and edge deployment.}
CrisisMMD~\cite{alam2018crisismmd} and FloodNet~\cite{rahnemoonfar2021floodnet}
support multimodal disaster analysis. Lightweight vision and language models
such as MobileNetV3~\cite{howard2019mobilenetv3} and
DistilBERT~\cite{sanh2019distilbert} motivate on-device extraction, while
EmergencyNet~\cite{kyrkou2020emergencynet} illustrates embedded inference.
Our experiments do not benchmark these models: flood level and urgency are
provided by the generator. Edge extraction is therefore deployment context,
not an empirical contribution of this paper.

\paragraph{Graph clustering and spatial constraints.}
Louvain~\cite{blondel2008louvain} and Leiden~\cite{traag2019leiden} optimize
weighted modularity without a preset cluster count. K-Means
~\cite{macqueen1967some}, DBSCAN~\cite{ester1996dbscan}, HDBSCAN
~\cite{campello2013hdbscan}, and spectral clustering
~\cite{vonluxburg2007spectral} provide complementary baselines. Spatial
clustering is also addressed explicitly by ClustGeo
~\cite{chavent2018clustgeo}, contiguity-constrained regionalization
~\cite{assuncao2006efficient}, and broader spatially constrained methods
~\cite{yuan2021spatial}. These lines make clear that neither multiplying kernels
nor enforcing compact geography is new in isolation.

\paragraph{Product kernels and positioning.}
Bilateral filtering multiplies spatial and range kernels so either factor can
veto an interaction~\cite{tomasi1998bilateral}. We transpose that mechanism
from pixels to rescue reports. The additive form used below is a controlled
ablation constructed by us, not a claimed representation of all prior spatial
clustering. Table~\ref{tab:positioning} locates the narrower contribution.

\paragraph{Priority and equity.}
Multi-criteria emergency allocation has long treated objectives and weights as
policy choices~\cite{saaty1980ahp,vitoriano2011multicriteria}. Humanitarian
logistics also exposes genuine tension among equity, efficiency, and efficacy
~\cite{gralla2014review,huang2012models}. Our vulnerability multiplier is an
explicit, bounded policy control; outcome evaluation is required because merely
changing a ranking does not demonstrate benefit.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Report Attributes and Deployment Scope}

Each report is represented as
\[
v_i=(L_i,T_i,F_i,E_i,N_i,V_i,C_i),
\]
where $L_i$ and $T_i$ are location and time, $F_i,E_i\in[0,1]$ are flood level
and urgency, $N_i$ is reported population, $V_i\ge0$ is vulnerability mass, and
$C_i\in(0,1)$ is confidence. A compact JSON representation occupies 105--111
bytes in our serialization experiment. A prospective mobile client can extract
$F_i$ and $E_i$ using lightweight image/text models and send only these
attributes. Because no device latency, memory, energy, or model-accuracy result
is reported here, this remains a proposed deployment architecture.

Confidence is the interpretable heuristic
\[
C_i=\operatorname{sigmoid}\!\left(b_0+b_1{\bf1}[\mathrm{image}]
              +b_2\log(1+n_{\mathrm{corrob},i})\right).
\]
It downweights inputs to the priority score; it is not treated as a validated
misinformation detector.

\subsection{Product-Kernel Graph and Localization Guarantee}

For reports $i,j$, let $d_{ij}$ be Haversine distance and
$\Delta t_{ij}$ the absolute time difference. We define
\begin{align}
S_{\mathrm{geo}}(d)&=\exp[-d^2/(2\sigma^2)],\\
S_{\mathrm{temp}}(\Delta t)&=\exp[-\Delta t/\tau_t],\\
S_{\mathrm{ctx}}(i,j)&=\exp[-|F_i-F_j|/\tau_F-|E_i-E_j|/\tau_E],\\
w_{ij}^{\times}&=S_{\mathrm{geo}}(d_{ij})
 [\beta S_{\mathrm{temp}}+\gamma S_{\mathrm{ctx}}],
\label{eq:product}
\end{align}
with $\beta,\gamma\ge0$ and $\beta+\gamma\le1$. We retain weights above
$\theta$ and then keep the union of each node's $k$ strongest edges. The
$k$-nearest operation can only delete edges, so the following result survives
both sparsification stages.

\begin{lemma}[Localization of product-kernel edges]\label{lem:localization}
For $0<\theta<1$, every retained edge under~\eqref{eq:product} satisfies
\[
d_{ij}<r_\theta:=\sigma\sqrt{2\ln(1/\theta)}.
\]
If any two vertices in a connected component are joined by a path of at most
$h$ edges, that component has geographic diameter below $h r_\theta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since both similarities are at most one and $\beta+\gamma\le1$,
$w_{ij}^{\times}\le S_{\mathrm{geo}}(d_{ij})$. Thus
$w_{ij}^{\times}>\theta$ implies
$\exp[-d_{ij}^2/(2\sigma^2)]>\theta$, which is equivalent to
$d_{ij}<\sigma\sqrt{2\ln(1/\theta)}$. Applying the triangle inequality along
a path of at most $h$ retained edges gives the component bound.
\end{proof}

For $\sigma=700$\,m and $\theta=0.05$, every retained edge is shorter than
1.713\,km. This also enables safe spatial blocking: pairs outside $r_\theta$
need not be evaluated when constructing the graph.

\paragraph{Why addition has no analogous guarantee.}
The controlled baseline is
\[
w_{ij}^{+}=\alpha S_{\mathrm{geo}}+
            \beta S_{\mathrm{temp}}+\gamma S_{\mathrm{ctx}}.
\]
Its non-geographic terms create a positive floor independent of distance.
Because $F,E\in[0,1]$, for example,
$\gamma S_{\mathrm{ctx}}\ge
\gamma\exp(-1/\tau_F-1/\tau_E)>0$ when $\gamma>0$. For any threshold below
that floor, equally timed or context-compatible reports can remain connected
at arbitrary geographic separation. This does not say additive weighting
cannot form compact clusters after calibration; it says compactness is not
guaranteed by $\theta$ and $\sigma$ alone.

Product gating has zero violations in 38 thresholds. The additive ablation

\subsection{Community Detection and Cluster Priority}

Louvain partitions the sparse weighted graph; Leiden is checked as a
drop-in alternative. For cluster $C_k$, we compute
\begin{align}
\widetilde E_k &= |C_k|^{-1}\sum_{i\in C_k}E_iC_i, &
\widetilde F_k &= \max_{i\in C_k}(F_iC_i),\\
\widetilde N_k &= \min\!\left\{1,
\frac{\log(1+\sum_{i\in C_k}N_iC_i)}{\log(1+N_{\mathrm{ref}})}\right\},&
A_k &=1+(\mu-1)\tanh\!\left(\frac{\sum_{i\in C_k}V_i}{s}\right),\\
R_k&=\omega_E\widetilde E_k+\omega_F\widetilde F_k+
\omega_N\widetilde N_k,&
\mathcal P(C_k)&=A_kR_k.
\end{align}
Weights $\boldsymbol\omega$ sum to one and $\mu\in[1,2]$, so
$\mathcal P\in[0,2)$. We use a static $N_{\mathrm{ref}}=500$ rather than the
largest current cluster. With a dynamic reference, an unchanged cluster's
score can change when an unrelated larger cluster appears.

\section{Experimental Design}

\subsection{Hard Synthetic Benchmark}

The seed-42 dataset contains 485 reports: 421 labeled observations from 13
physical events, 60 noise reports, and a four-report fake campaign. Event sizes
and spreads vary from 8--70 observations and 120--900\,m. Three event pairs
have overlapping spatial envelopes but contrasting $(F,E)$ profiles; another
pair shares a location but is separated by 3.5\,h; one label is bimodal.
Thirty-nine reports are fake, 66.7\% of them are placed inside event regions,
and image availability overlaps between real and fake reports.

\subsection{Parameters, Metrics, and Statistical Protocol}

Defaults are $\sigma=700$\,m, $\tau_t=45$\,min,
$(\tau_F,\tau_E)=(0.25,0.35)$, $(\beta,\gamma)=(0.5,0.5)$,
$\theta=0.05$, $k=12$, resolution $1.0$,
$(\omega_E,\omega_F,\omega_N)=(0.34,0.33,0.33)$, $s=10$, and $\mu=2$.
We report ARI/NMI only on labeled points. The primary geographic statistics
are computed identically for every method over predicted clusters containing
at least one labeled point; noise-only clusters and unassigned points are
reported separately. This prevents a method's noise-bin convention from
determining its apparent diameter.

Twenty-seed tests regenerate inter-event geometry, group sizes, and spreads
(not only point jitter). Paired differences use bootstrap 95\% confidence
intervals and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. A result is called different only if
the paired interval excludes zero.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Clustering Across Regenerated Geometries}

At the shared operational default $\theta=0.05$, product gating has mean ARI
$0.9616\pm0.0134$ (95\% CI $[0.9562,0.9675]$), versus
$0.9348\pm0.0179$ ($[0.9272,0.9426]$) for additive $\alpha=1$.
The paired ARI difference is 0.0268, CI $[0.0198,0.0345]$,
$p=1.32\times10^{-4}$. Mean labeled-cluster diameter is 1.54\,km versus
98.53\,km. These numbers characterize the shared default, not intrinsic
superiority under every calibrated threshold.

\subsection{Baselines and Measurement Convention}

On seed 42, Louvain and Leiden coincide (ARI 0.9515, mean labeled diameter
1.50\,km). Agglomerative clustering on the same distance ties them but is given
$K=52$, the count discovered by Louvain. HDBSCAN reaches nearly the same ARI
(0.9508), but its 15 labeled clusters average 6.58\,km and reach 79.86\,km;
three additional clusters contain only noise. Coordinate-only K-Means reaches
ARI 0.6304. Thus the evidence supports the weighted graph's partition and
automatic $K$, not a universal claim that Louvain is superior.

Removing context lowers global ARI from 0.9515 to 0.7775 and overlapping-pair

\subsection{Threshold Calibration and Lemma Verification}

A shared numerical threshold is not a scale-invariant comparison: at
$\theta=0.05$, product gating retains 10.85\% of pairs while additive variants
retain 95.69--97.97\%. Figure~\ref{fig:default} is therefore a diagnostic of
the default, not evidence that addition can never produce compact clusters.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.9\textwidth]{figures/fig1_ablation.png}
\caption{Shared-default diagnostic. Additive weights form near-complete graphs
at $\theta=0.05$; their large diameters here must not be read as an intrinsic
limit of addition. The right panel illustrates confidence weighting, not
misinformation detection.}\label{fig:default}
\end{figure}

We calibrate each form separately using an operational criterion that does not
use labels: maximum diameter below 5\,km, fewer than half of events in
singletons, and 5--40 multi-report clusters. Additive $\alpha=1$ has 37 usable
grid points versus 34 for product gating and reaches a 3.17\,km maximum
diameter at its best-ARI threshold. Consequently, there is no defensible
``wider window'' contribution. The invariant result is
Lemma~\ref{lem:localization}: zero measured violations for product gating, while
all additive variants violate the analogous bound.

\subsection{Priority Stability and Dispatch Outcomes}

Perturbing $\boldsymbol\omega$ by $\pm0.10$ yields mean Kendall
$\tau=0.9665$ (minimum 0.9276), but the top-three set is preserved in 87\%,
not 100\%, of trials. Structural perturbations of $\sigma$ yield matched-cluster
$\tau\ge0.989$. In an online stress sequence, the static population reference
keeps anchor-cluster priority unchanged ($\tau=1$, zero score drift); a dynamic
maximum ends at $\tau=0.9111$ with maximum absolute priority drift 0.1265.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.70\textwidth]{figures/fig7_ranking_stability.png}
\caption{Ranking stability under 200 weight perturbations per level. Kendall
$\tau$ measures self-consistency, not correctness.}\label{fig:rank}
\end{figure}

The dispatch simulation serves only clusters with at least two reports and one
report with $C_i\ge0.5$, uses three boats, three depot positions, and 20
regenerated geometries. Its primary, formula-external metric weights arrival
time by vulnerable population only in clusters with $F_{\max}>0.7$. Product
vulnerability amplification improves on omitting vulnerability by 14.82\,min
(paired CI $[-26.68,-3.73]$, $p=0.0266$). Against additive vulnerability, the
difference is only $-1.43$\,min with CI $[-10.91,7.88]$ and $p=0.756$.
Therefore vulnerability-aware scoring helps relative to ignoring vulnerability,
but the data do not establish that multiplication is better than addition.

\subsection{Confidence Heuristic and Computational Cost}

The combined fake score $1-C_i$ has AUC 0.6919
([0.6095,0.7693]) and AP 0.1546 ([0.1135,0.2267]) against random AP 0.0804.
The single feature $-n_{\mathrm{corrob}}$ has slightly lower AUC (0.6903) but
higher AP (0.1680). Conditional AUC is 0.421, 0.468, and 0.623 for
$n_{\mathrm{corrob}}=0$, 1--5, and $>5$, respectively. The four coordinated
fake reports attain mean $C_i=0.9273$, above the real-report mean 0.9035.
Hence $C_i$ is retained only as a transparent downweighting convention.

Constructing the dense weight matrix is $O(n^2)$; Louvain's favorable sparse
complexity does not change the end-to-end bound. In the recorded run, 7,200
events take 46.06\,s on one CPU core, of which the vectorized matrix build takes
33.76\,s. Lemma~\ref{lem:localization} suggests a scalable implementation:
spatial indexing can omit all pairs outside $r_\theta$ before evaluating the
other kernels.

\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}

\paragraph{What is supported.}
The product form gives a closed-form, data-independent edge-localization
guarantee and strong shared-default performance on a benchmark where coordinates
alone do not solve the task. Context has a measurable role specifically on
overlapping events. Static population normalization prevents unrelated score
drift. These statements have direct analytic or multi-seed support.

\paragraph{What is not supported.}
Per-form calibration shows additive weighting can also produce compact,
high-ARI partitions; the contribution is not a threshold-window ratio or
universal empirical dominance. The confidence heuristic is not a reliable fake
report detector. Product vulnerability amplification is not significantly
better than additive vulnerability on the primary outcome. Edge-model accuracy
and on-device resource use are not evaluated.

\paragraph{Validity limits.}
The benchmark is synthetic and covers one regional flood scenario. Its
ground-truth physical events and hard cases are designed rather than observed.
Twenty geometries quantify generator variability but cannot replace field data.
ARI/NMI score label agreement, whereas dispatch simulation depends on a simple
greedy queue, fixed boat speed, and fixed service time. HDBSCAN and DBSCAN
remain sensitive to their own hyperparameters, while Louvain depends on
resolution, $\theta$, and $k$. A real-data sanity check using geolocated,
time-resolved flood reports is the highest-priority next step.

\section{Conclusion}

We presented a product-kernel weighted graph and a bounded cluster-priority
score for flood-rescue coordination. The main contribution is a localization
lemma: thresholded product edges, and therefore connected paths, have an
explicit geographic bound controlled by $\sigma$ and $\theta$. A redesigned
synthetic benchmark confirms that geography alone is insufficient and that
context separates spatially overlapping events. Multi-seed results favor
product gating at the shared operational default, while per-form calibration
correctly narrows the empirical claim. Priority outcomes support including
vulnerability but do not distinguish multiplicative from additive treatment;
confidence scoring remains heuristic. Field validation and sparse,
spatial-indexed graph construction are required before deployment.
